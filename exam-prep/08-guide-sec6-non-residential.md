## 6.1 Commercial roof assemblies and approvals

### The layered assembly, bottom to top
1. Structural deck (steel, concrete, wood, LWIC, gypsum)
2. **Thermal barrier / substrate board** (e.g., gypsum over steel, where the code or the tested assembly requires it)
3. **Vapor retarder / air barrier** (when needed)
4. **Insulation**: one or more layers, staggered joints
5. **Cover board**
6. **Membrane**
7. **Surfacing/protection**: coating, granules, ballast, pavers, or a vegetative system

### Ratings you must recognize
| Rating | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **FM Approvals, Class 1 + uplift class** (e.g., **1-60, 1-75, 1-90, 1-120**) | The assembly passed FM fire and wind testing. The number is the **uplift rating in psf** (tested with a safety factor). The insurer (FM Global) may require it |
| **UL 790 / ASTM E108 Class A, B, C** | **External fire** resistance of the roof covering. **Class A** is the highest |
| **UL 580 / UL 1897** | Wind-uplift test methods |
| **ASCE 7** | Calculates the **design wind pressures** (field, perimeter, corner zones) the assembly must resist |
| **ANSI/SPRI ES-1** | Test standard for **roof edge metal** (fascia/coping). The model building code requires it for most low-slope membrane roofs **[JURISDICTION]** |
| **Energy code (IECC / Illinois Energy Conservation Code)** | Minimum roof R-values. Reroofing that exposes the insulation often triggers upgrades **[JURISDICTION/EDITION]** |
| **Cool roof (reflectance/emittance)** | Rated by the **CRRC**. Some jurisdictions (e.g., **Chicago**) require reflective low-slope roofs **[JURISDICTION]** |

**Decision tested (INFERENCE):** "The specification calls for an FM 1-90 assembly, and the crew proposes spacing insulation fasteners farther apart than the approved pattern." Best answer: **install exactly per the approved assembly and fastening pattern.** A field change voids the rating unless it is approved.

## 6.2 Steel decks and insulation attachment

| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Steel deck | Commonly **1½-in wide-rib (Type B)**, **22 ga minimum** in NRCA/FM practice. It is attached to joists by **puddle welds, screws, or power-actuated fasteners**, and sidelaps are fastened. **Reinforce or repair rusted or deflected areas before reroofing.** Report deck damage to the owner (structural) |
| Insulation over steel | Must **span the flutes**. Keep board **edges bearing on the top flanges**. Stagger joints between layers |
| Mechanical fastening | **Screws + stress plates** set by the **uplift zone** (e.g., more fasteners per 4 × 8 board in perimeters and corners). Fasteners must **penetrate the top flange of the steel** by the manufacturer's minimum (commonly **¾ in**) **[MANUFACTURER/FM]** |
| Adhesive attachment | **Low-rise polyurethane foam adhesive** in **beads** (e.g., 12 in o.c. in the field, closer in perimeters and corners) or full-coverage spray. Boards are "walked in" or weighted until the adhesive sets **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| Hot asphalt | Only for compatible boards (polyiso, perlite, fiberboard, cellular glass). **Never directly on polystyrene** |
| Over-driven/under-driven fasteners | Over-driven plates dish the board and create low spots. **Under-driven** heads stand proud and puncture the membrane ("fastener back-out") |
| **Fastener back-out** | Cyclic deck movement or wind vibration pushes screws up → **punctures the membrane from below**. Look for "tented" bumps in the membrane over fasteners |

## 6.3 Vapor control in commercial and industrial buildings

**High-humidity occupancies always need evaluation:**
- natatoriums (pools);
- laundries;
- paper mills;
- food processing;
- breweries;
- textile and wet manufacturing;
- **cold-storage** (freezer) buildings, where the vapor drive **reverses**: warm, moist air outside, cold inside.

**Rules:**
- **General rule:** the vapor retarder goes on the **warm side** of the insulation.
- **Over steel deck:** fasten or adhere a **substrate board** first, apply the vapor retarder on top of it, and then install the insulation over it.
- **Continuity matters:** seal the vapor retarder at perimeters and penetrations and tie it into the wall air/vapor barrier. **Air leakage at the roof-wall joint is a leading cause of condensation problems** [2026 NRCA volume emphasis].
- **Cold storage:** the vapor retarder goes on the **exterior (warm) side** of the insulation, **which is usually the roof membrane itself**. The assembly must be designed so that no interior vapor retarder traps moisture.

## 6.4 Metal panel roofs (commercial and industrial)

| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Structural standing seam (SSR)** | Spans **purlins** (no solid deck needed). **Concealed clips**: **fixed** clips at one point (often the eave, ridge, or midpoint, per design) and **sliding clips** elsewhere, so panels move with temperature. **Mechanically field-seamed** (e.g., 180° double-lock) for low slopes |
| **Architectural standing seam** | Needs a **solid substrate** and underlayment. Steeper slopes (it sheds water) |
| **Through-fastened ("R-panel")** | Common on **pre-engineered metal buildings**. Screws with sealing washers through the panel into purlins. Side laps need sealant tape on low slopes |
| Min slopes | Standing seam **¼:12**; lapped with sealant **½:12**; lapped without sealant **3:12** **[JURISDICTION: IRC/IBC]** |
| Panel end laps | Sealed with tape mastic; laps in the direction of water flow; fastened per the manufacturer |
| **Thermal movement** | Long runs move significantly (see the §3.13 calculation). **Transition and ridge details must allow movement** (the ridge closure is attached to one panel and slides at the other) |
| **Condensation under metal** | Metal buildings: **faced blanket insulation** with the **vapor-retarder facing toward the warm (interior) side**. Taped seams. A **thermal spacer block** at the purlins to reduce the thermal short |
| **Metal-over-metal retrofit** | New framing ("sub-purlins" or retrofit framing) over the existing panels. The structural capacity is checked **[engineer]** |
| **Coatings on metal roofs** | Clean, remove rust, **seal fasteners and laps**, prime per the manufacturer, then coat |

## 6.5 Spray polyurethane foam (SPF) and coatings

| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| What it is | Two components (**A side: isocyanate; B side: polyol resin**) mixed at the gun. It expands into a seamless, adhered, insulating layer. Roofing-grade foam is roughly **2.5–3.0 lb/ft³** density **[MANUFACTURER/ASTM C1029]** |
| Protective coating | **Required.** SPF degrades in UV. Use an elastomeric coating (**acrylic, silicone, urethane**) or aggregate. Apply the coating **within the manufacturer's window** (often the same day or within about 72 hours) **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| Weather limits | **Don't spray on wet or damp surfaces**, in high wind (overspray and poor texture), or when the substrate is near the **dew point**. A common rule is surface temperature **at least 5°F above the dew point** **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| Surface texture | **Smooth / orange peel / coarse orange peel** are acceptable to coat. **Popcorn / tree bark** is **unacceptable**: poor coating coverage and water holding. Remove it and re-spray |
| Thickness | Specified for R-value **and to create slope** (SPF can be sprayed thicker to build drainage) |
| **Overspray** | Travels far in the wind. Protect cars, windows, and HVAC (masking, wind screens, stopping work in wind) |
| Safety | **Isocyanate** is a respiratory **sensitizer**. Use supplied-air or appropriate respirators, controlled zones, and notify occupants. Shut down or seal **HVAC intakes** |
| Maintenance | Inspect the coating; recoat at the end of its service life. **Blisters in SPF** come from moisture, poor pass adhesion, or a wet substrate |

**Roof coatings in general (restoration):**
- Clean.
- Repair wet areas: **wet insulation must be removed first**; don't coat over wet roofs.
- Prime as required.
- Reinforce seams and flashings with fabric.
- Apply at the specified **dry-film thickness** (mils). Coverage is in gallons per square, and wet mils ≠ dry mils (solids content matters) **[MANUFACTURER]**.

### Worked calculation: coating coverage
A coating is **60% solids by volume**. The specification requires **20 dry mils**.
1. Wet mils needed = 20 ÷ 0.60 = **33.3 wet mils**
2. 1 gallon spread over 100 sq ft gives about **16 wet mils** (1 gal = 231 in³; 231 ÷ 14,400 in² = 0.016 in = 16 mils)
3. Gallons per square = 33.3 ÷ 16 = **≈ 2.08 gal/sq** (before waste)
4. For **120 squares**: 2.08 × 120 = **≈ 250 gal** (+ waste)

## 6.6 Rooftop equipment, traffic, and other commercial details

| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Equipment supports** | Equipment goes on **curbs** (flashed like any curb, **≥ 8 in**) or on **raised supports** tall enough to allow reroofing and maintenance underneath **[NRCA-P]**. NRCA publishes a table of minimum clearances **that increases with equipment width** (e.g., about 14 in for narrow units up to about 48 in for very wide ones). **Learn the principle: wider equipment needs more clearance**, because the roofer must reach under it |
| **Walkway pads** | On service routes, at ladders and hatches, and around equipment, to protect the membrane |
| **Roof hatches / ladders** | Guarded per OSHA when open (a hatch is a hole) |
| **Lightning protection** | Coordinate with the lightning contractor. Cable anchors are **adhered or mechanically attached per the membrane manufacturer**, never through-fastened randomly |
| **PV (solar) arrays** | Loads, wind, and fire access pathways. Attachments flashed like penetrations. Ballast checked structurally. Coordinate the membrane warranty |
| **Vegetative (green) roofs** | Waterproofing + **root barrier** + drainage/protection + growing medium + plants. **Heavy** (saturated weight is often **15–50+ psf**). **Leak-test before the overburden goes on** (flood or electronic) |
| **Ballasted and paver systems** | Stone ~10–12 psf; **pavers ~18–24 psf** or more. **Check structural capacity** |
| **Protected membrane roof (PMR / IRMA)** | The membrane goes **under** the insulation: **XPS** (low water absorption) over the membrane, then filter fabric and ballast/pavers. The membrane is protected from UV, traffic, and temperature swings. **Insulation must be XPS** (it will get wet periodically) |
| **Expansion joints** | Raised curb-type joints at building expansion joints. Never simply membrane across them |
| **Parapets and copings** | Coping sloped toward the roof, with joints and cleats. The membrane runs up and over the parapet, or terminates under the coping per the detail |

## 6.7 Waterproofing (not roofing, but in the references)

**NRCA Waterproofing Manual scope (NOT READ): below-grade walls, plaza decks, and split-slab construction.**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Waterproofing vs. dampproofing** | **Waterproofing** resists water **under hydrostatic pressure**. **Dampproofing** only resists moisture/vapor **without** hydrostatic head |
| **Positive-side vs. negative-side** | **Positive side** = the side where water comes from (outside of a foundation wall). **Negative side** = the interior face. **Membranes belong on the positive side**. Negative-side systems are limited (e.g., cementitious crystalline products) |
| **Hot fluid-applied rubberized asphalt** | Common plaza-deck membrane, applied in layers (often a total of ~215 mils with fabric reinforcement) **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| **Protection course / board** | Protects the membrane from backfill and construction damage |
| **Drainage composite / drainage mat** | Relieves hydrostatic pressure and carries water to drains |
| **Flood test** | Before the overburden goes on, the membrane is **flood-tested** by damming and holding water (e.g., per **ASTM D5957**; commonly **24–72 h**, with ~**2 in** of water at the high point) **[verify the spec]** |
| **Bentonite** | Clay panels that swell when wet. Must be confined; not for exposed or freeze-thaw surfaces |
| **Split-slab (plaza)** | Membrane on the structural slab, then protection/drainage/insulation, then a wearing slab or pavers |

## 6.8 Working on occupied commercial buildings, warranties, and quality control
- **Fumes and odors** (hot asphalt, adhesives, SPF): coordinate with the owner. **Shut down or seal rooftop air intakes** during work, and use low-odor products where needed.
- **Tear-off in sections that can be made watertight the same day.** Install **water cut-offs / night seals**, and remove them before continuing (§2.6).
- **Asbestos** in old roofs: test before tear-off (§1.10).
- **Deck damage** found during tear-off: stop, document, and notify. Unit-price items for deck replacement are common in commercial contracts.
- **Warranties:**
  - **Manufacturer:** material-only, system, or **NDL** (no dollar limit). These often require a **manufacturer inspection** and approved-applicator status.
  - **Contractor workmanship:** commonly **2 years** by industry convention; per the contract.
  - Warranties usually **exclude** damage from ponding beyond limits, unauthorized alterations, and lack of maintenance.
- **Quality control:** daily **test welds** (§4.5), seam probing, adhesive coverage checks, fastener pull-out tests (existing decks), core cuts, and **infrared or electronic leak detection** at completion.

> **WHAT TO MEMORIZE (Section 6)**
> FM **1-60/1-90** = uplift psf class · UL 790/E108 **Class A** best · ASCE 7 zones: corner > perimeter > field · **SPRI ES-1** edges · steel deck **22 ga** min, 1½-in wide rib, insulation spans flutes, fasteners **¾ in** through the flange
> Low-rise foam beads, no hot asphalt on polystyrene · fastener back-out punctures the membrane · vapor retarder on the **warm side**: over a substrate board on steel; **cold storage reverses it**
> SSR: fixed + sliding clips; faced blanket with the facing to the **warm side**; min slopes ¼ / ½ / 3 : 12
> SPF: A = isocyanate, B = polyol; ~2.5–3.0 pcf; **coat for UV**; no spraying when wet or near the **dew point (≥ 5°F above)**; popcorn/tree bark = reject · coating wet mils = dry mils ÷ volume solids; 1 gal/sq ≈ 16 wet mils
> PMR: **XPS over the membrane** + ballast · green roofs: root barrier, heavy, **leak-test first** · pavers ~18–24 psf, stone ~10–12 psf
> Waterproofing = **hydrostatic** (dampproofing isn't) · **positive side** · protection board + drainage composite · **flood test before overburden**

> **HOW TO REASON THROUGH NON-RESIDENTIAL ITEMS**
> - Commercial answers favor **tested assemblies and approvals** (FM, UL, the manufacturer's spec). "Do it the way the approval says" beats "do what works."
> - Look for **loads** (ballast, pavers, equipment, green roofs → **check structure**), **movement** (expansion joints, clips, thermal), and **air and vapor** (occupancy humidity, cold storage).
> - When the building is **occupied**, the best answer protects the **people inside** (air intakes, fumes, water cut-offs) as well as the roof.
