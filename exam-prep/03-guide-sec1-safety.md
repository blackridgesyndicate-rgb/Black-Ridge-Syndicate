## 1.1 Fall protection: the framework (the most-tested safety topic)

### Plain English
Anyone working **6 ft or more above a lower level** on a construction site needs fall protection. What counts as acceptable depends on the **roof slope**, the **type of work**, and whether it's **residential construction**.

### Terms you must recognize (29 CFR 1926.500(b) definitions) [OSHA]
- **Low-slope roof:** slope **≤ 4 in 12** (vertical to horizontal).
- **Steep roof:** slope **> 4 in 12**.
- **Roofing work:** hoisting, storage, application, and removal of roofing materials and equipment, including related insulation, sheet metal, and vapor-barrier work. It **does not** include constructing the roof deck.
- **Unprotected sides and edges:** any side or edge where there is no wall or guardrail at least 39 in high.
- **Hole:** a gap or void **2 in or more** in its least dimension.
- **Competent person** (1926.32(f)): capable of identifying hazards **and** has authority to take prompt corrective measures.
- **Qualified person** (1926.32(m)): has a recognized degree, certificate, or professional standing, or extensive knowledge, training, and experience, and can solve problems relating to the subject.

### Which paragraph applies? (1926.501(b)) [OSHA]

| Situation | Paragraph | Permitted protection |
|---|---|---|
| Any unprotected side/edge ≥ 6 ft | (b)(1) | Guardrail, safety net, or personal fall arrest system (PFAS) |
| Holes, **including skylights** | (b)(4) | Covers, guardrails, or PFAS. Skylights count as holes. |
| **Roofing work on low-slope roofs** with edges ≥ 6 ft above lower level | **(b)(10)** | Guardrail, safety net, or PFAS, **or a combination** of: warning line + guardrail; warning line + safety net; warning line + PFAS; **warning line + safety monitoring**. **On roofs 50 ft or less in width, safety monitoring alone is permitted.** |
| **Steep roofs** with edges ≥ 6 ft | **(b)(11)** | Guardrail **with toeboards**, safety net, or PFAS. **No warning lines, no safety monitor.** |
| **Residential construction** ≥ 6 ft | **(b)(13)** | Guardrail, safety net, or PFAS. If the employer can show these are **infeasible or create a greater hazard**, a written **fall protection plan** per 1926.502(k) may be used. |

**What the roofer sees and what the question tests:** a 3-story flat roof, 40 ft wide, reroof crew → (b)(10), and monitor-only is legal because width ≤ 50 ft. A 6:12 house roof → (b)(11) or (b)(13) → PFAS is the typical answer. "Slide guards/roof brackets alone" are **not** conventional fall protection. OSHA's old interim residential guidance that allowed slide guards was rescinded (STD 03-11-002, 2010). [OSHA]

### Common wrong answers and why they tempt
| Wrong answer | Why it's tempting | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|---|
| "Warning line alone" on a low-slope roof | Warning lines are roof-specific | A warning line only marks an area; outside it (and at the edge) you need another system. It is never a stand-alone system |
| "Safety monitor alone" on a 75-ft-wide low-slope roof | Monitors are allowed on low-slope roofs | Alone only if width ≤ 50 ft. Otherwise it must be combined with a warning line |
| "Safety monitor" on a 6:12 roof | Workers are close to the edge | (b)(11) permits only guardrail + toeboards, net, or PFAS |
| "Fall protection starts at 10 ft" | That's the **scaffold** threshold (1926.451(g)) | Roofs/edges: **6 ft** |
| "Low slope means under 3:12" | Industry sometimes calls ≤3:12 low slope | OSHA's line is **4:12**. 4:12 itself is low-slope (≤) |

## 1.2 Warning lines, safety monitors, guardrails, nets, covers [OSHA-V for 1926.502]

### Warning line systems (1926.502(f))
| Requirement | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance from roof edge, **no mechanical equipment** | **≥ 6 ft** |
| With mechanical equipment | **≥ 6 ft** from edges **parallel** to direction of equipment travel; **≥ 10 ft** from edges **perpendicular** to it |
| Height of line (lowest point incl. sag / highest point) | **34 in min / 39 in max** |
| Flagging | High-visibility material at **≤ 6-ft intervals** |
| Stanchion strength | Must resist **16 lb** applied horizontally, **30 in** above surface, toward the edge, without tipping |
| Rope/wire/chain tensile strength | **≥ 500 lb** |
| Access paths | Access, materials handling, storage, and hoisting areas connect to the work area by a **path formed by two warning lines**. When the path is not in use, close it with a rope/wire/chain equal in strength and height, or offset it so no one can walk straight into the work area |
| No work between line and edge | Unless protected by another system (guardrail, net, PFAS, or safety monitor) |
| Mechanical equipment (1926.502(f)(3)) | Used or stored **only** where employees are protected by warning line, guardrail, or PFAS. **Not in a monitor-only area** |

### Safety monitoring systems (1926.502(h))
The monitor must be a **competent person** who:
- recognizes fall hazards and warns employees when they are unaware of a hazard or acting unsafely;
- is on the **same walking/working surface** and within **visual sighting distance**;
- is **close enough to communicate orally**;
- has **no other responsibilities** that could take the monitor's attention away.

Other rules:
- **No mechanical equipment** is used or stored in monitored areas on low-slope roofs.
- **Only employees doing roofing work** (or covered by a fall protection plan) may be in a monitored area.
- Employees must comply promptly with the monitor's warnings.

### Guardrail systems (1926.502(b))
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Top rail height | **42 in ± 3 in** (39–45 in) |
| Midrail | Midway between top rail and surface (for a 42-in top rail, ≈ 21 in) |
| Top rail strength | **200 lb** outward/downward within 2 in of top edge; under that load it may not deflect below **39 in** |
| Midrail strength | **150 lb** |
| Toeboard | **3½ in** high min; **50 lb** strength; required where objects could fall. **Required on steep roofs** in (b)(11) |
| Wire rope top rail | Flagged at ≤ 6 ft; ≥ ¼ in diameter |
| At hoisting areas | Chain, gate, or removable section across the access opening when hoisting is not taking place |

### Safety nets (1926.502(c))
- As close as practicable under the work surface, and **never more than 30 ft below** it.
- Horizontal extension beyond the edge: **8 ft** (net ≤ 5 ft below), **10 ft** (5–10 ft below), **13 ft** (> 10 ft below).
- Drop test: **400-lb bag of sand**, 30 ± 2 in diameter.
- Mesh openings: ≤ **36 sq in** and ≤ **6 in** on a side.

### Covers (1926.502(i))
- Support **at least twice** the weight of employees, equipment, and materials that may be imposed.
- **Secured** against displacement.
- Marked **"HOLE" or "COVER"** (or color-coded).
- Vehicle-traffic covers: twice the axle load.
- **Skylights:** screens or covers rated for the load, or guardrails, or PFAS. **A plastic skylight dome is NOT a cover.**

### Falling-object protection and materials near edges (1926.502(j)) [OSHA]
- During roofing work, materials and equipment **must not be stored within 6 ft of a roof edge unless guardrails are erected at the edge.**
- Materials piled near a roof edge must be stable and self-supporting.
- (Contrast: the **4-ft** storage rule in 1926.502(j) is for **overhand bricklaying/masonry**, a common distractor.)

## 1.3 Personal fall arrest systems (PFAS) (1926.502(d)) [OSHA]

### Plain English
A harness, a connector (lanyard, self-retracting lifeline, or rope grab on a lifeline), and an anchor. It stops a fall that has already started. **Positioning** and **restraint** are different: they keep you from falling (or limit it to 2 ft).

### Numbers to memorize
| Component / criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Anchorage** | **5,000 lb per employee attached**, OR designed, installed, and used as part of a complete PFAS with a **safety factor of at least 2**, under supervision of a **qualified person** |
| Connectors (D-rings, snaphooks) | Min tensile **5,000 lb**; proof-tested to **3,600 lb** |
| Snaphooks | **Locking type** only (non-locking prohibited since 1998) |
| Lanyards and vertical lifelines | Min breaking strength **5,000 lb** |
| Self-retracting lifelines/lanyards that limit free fall to ≤ 2 ft | **3,000 lb** min; otherwise 5,000 lb |
| Vertical lifelines | **One employee per lifeline** |
| **Max arresting force** (with body harness) | **1,800 lb** |
| **Max deceleration distance** | **3.5 ft** |
| **Max free fall** | **6 ft**, and the worker must **not contact any lower level** |
| Body belts | **Not permitted** as part of a PFAS (since Jan 1, 1998). Allowed only for positioning |
| After a fall | Remove from service until **inspected by a competent person** and found undamaged |
| Rescue | Employer must provide for **prompt rescue** or assure self-rescue |
| Inspection | **Before each use**, for wear, damage, and deterioration |
| Attachment points | Not to guardrails or hoists unless they meet anchorage criteria |
| Positioning device systems (1926.502(e)) | Free fall ≤ **2 ft**; anchorage ≥ **2× potential impact load or 3,000 lb**, whichever is greater |

### What the roofer sees / decision tested
- A roof anchor nailed through the sheathing only (not into a rafter/truss) → **inadequate anchorage.** Manufacturer instructions typically require fastening into framing **[MANUFACTURER]**.
- A 6-ft shock-absorbing lanyard clipped to an anchor at the worker's **feet** → free fall ≈ 6 ft + D-ring height (≈ 5 ft) ≈ **11 ft > 6-ft limit.** Correct fix: rig the anchor overhead, shorten the lanyard, or use an SRL/lanyard rated for foot-level anchorage. The best answer depends on the options, but the flaw is the **free-fall distance**.
- A worker fell and was arrested with no injury. **Best next step:** rescue promptly, then remove the harness and lanyard from service until a competent person inspects them. Do not simply reuse them.

### Worked calculation: required clearance below the anchor
*This is an industry method (ANSI/ASSP Z359 and manufacturer charts), not an OSHA formula. OSHA sets only the 6-ft free fall, 3.5-ft deceleration, and no-contact rules.*

Worker with a 6-ft shock-absorbing lanyard, anchor at the D-ring level (≈ 5 ft above feet):

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Free fall (lanyard length, anchor at D-ring height) | 6.0 ft |
| Deceleration distance (OSHA max) | 3.5 ft |
| Harness stretch / D-ring shift (industry allowance) | 1.0 ft |
| D-ring to bottom of feet | 5.0 ft |
| Safety margin (industry) | 2.0 ft |
| **Total clearance needed below anchor** | **17.5 ft** |

If the lower level is only 14 ft below the anchor, this system would let the worker strike the ground. Use an SRL, which reduces free fall to ≤ 2 ft (verify with the manufacturer's clearance chart).

### Common wrong answers
- "Anchorage 3,000 lb." That is the SRL (≤ 2 ft free fall) and positioning figure, not the general anchorage.
- "Free fall 4 ft." No; the limit is 6 ft.
- "Body belt is fine for arrest." Prohibited since 1998.
- "Two workers can share one vertical lifeline." Prohibited.

> **WHAT TO MEMORIZE**
> 6 ft trigger · 4:12 line (≤ low, > steep) · 50-ft width monitor-only · warning line 6 ft / 10 ft (equipment perpendicular) · 34–39 in line height · flags every 6 ft · 16 lb @ 30 in · 500 lb line
> Guardrail 42 ± 3 in, 200 lb / 150 lb midrail, toeboard 3½ in / 50 lb
> Anchorage 5,000 lb per person (or SF 2 by qualified person) · 1,800 lb arrest force · 3.5 ft decel · 6 ft free fall · SRL 3,000 lb · connectors 5,000 / 3,600 proof
> Net ≤ 30 ft below; 400-lb drop test · Covers 2× load, "HOLE"/"COVER" · no storage within 6 ft of edge without guardrail

> **HOW TO REASON THROUGH IT**
> 1) Is the edge ≥ 6 ft? 2) What is the slope: ≤ 4:12 or > 4:12? 3) Is it "roofing work" on a low-slope roof? If so, the menu is wider. 4) Roof width ≤ 50 ft? 5) Is mechanical equipment in use? 6) Is it residential construction? Then consider the fall protection plan path. 7) For a PFAS question, check anchorage, free fall, clearance, and component rules in that order.

## 1.4 Training and fall protection plans [OSHA]
- **1926.503:** the employer trains each employee exposed to fall hazards, through a **competent person**. Training covers hazard recognition, procedures for erecting, maintaining, disassembling, and inspecting systems, the monitor and warning-line roles, handling and storage of materials, and each employee's role.
- **Written certification record:** employee name, **date** of training, and **signature** of the trainer or employer.
- **Retraining** when: workplace changes make prior training obsolete, fall protection systems change, or an employee shows inadequate understanding or skill.
- **Fall protection plan (1926.502(k)):** allowed only for leading-edge work, precast concrete, and **residential construction**, when conventional protection is infeasible or creates a greater hazard. Requirements:
  - prepared by a **qualified person**, site-specific, kept current, with a copy **at the jobsite**;
  - changes approved by a qualified person, and implementation supervised by a **competent person**;
  - must document **why** conventional fall protection is infeasible, identify **controlled access zones**, and use a **safety monitoring system** where no other measure is used;
  - the employer must identify each employee designated to work in controlled access zones;
  - serious injuries and near misses require investigation and a plan review.

## 1.5 Ladders (29 CFR 1926 Subpart X) [OSHA]

| Rule | Value / cite |
|---|---|
| When a ladder/stairway is required | At a break in elevation of **19 in or more** with no ramp, runway, embankment, or hoist (1926.1051(a)) |
| Extension above landing | Side rails at least **3 ft above** the upper landing surface; if not possible, secure the ladder at top and provide a grab device (1926.1053(b)(1)) |
| Angle (non-self-supporting) | Horizontal distance from top support to foot ≈ **¼ of working length** (the **4:1 rule**) (1926.1053(b)(5)(i)) |
| Load capacity (portable) | Support at least **4×** max intended load (extra-heavy-duty type 1A metal or plastic: 3.3×) (1926.1053(a)(1)) |
| Rung spacing (portable) | **10–14 in** center-to-center (1926.1053(a)(3)(i)) |
| Stepladders | Do **not** use the top or top step as a step (1926.1053(b)(13)). Do not use a closed stepladder as a straight ladder |
| Near electrical equipment | **Nonconductive side rails** where the worker or ladder could contact exposed energized equipment (1926.1053(b)(12)) |
| Use | Face the ladder; at least **one hand** grasping; no load that could cause loss of balance (b)(20)-(22) |
| Footing | Stable, level surface unless secured; secure or barricade in traffic areas |
| Defects | Tag **"Do Not Use"** and remove from service (b)(16) |
| Training | By a competent person (1926.1060) |

Industry duty ratings (ANSI A14, not OSHA): Type **IAA 375 lb**, **IA 300 lb**, **I 250 lb**, II 225 lb, III 200 lb. Roofers carrying bundles need **IA or IAA**. [JURISDICTION: ANSI consensus standard]

### Worked calculation: ladder setup
Eave height 16 ft above grade. How far out should the foot be, and what working length is needed?
1. Foot distance = 16 ft ÷ 4 = **4 ft** from the wall.
2. Length to the eave = √(16² + 4²) = √(256 + 16) = √272 = **16.49 ft**.
3. Add 3 ft extension above the landing → **≈ 19.5 ft** working length.
4. Extension ladders lose length to section overlap (ANSI: ~3 ft overlap for ladders up to 36 ft). A **24-ft** extension ladder gives ≈ 21 ft working length, so it works. A 20-ft ladder gives ≈ 17 ft, which is too short.

### Common wrong answers
- "Extend 3 rungs" or "extend 36 in above the gutter" (36 in = 3 ft **is** correct. The trap is "3 rungs," which is only ~3 ft if rungs are 12 in apart, and is not the rule's wording).
- "1:3 angle." No, it's 1:4.
- "Aluminum ladder near power lines." Prohibited when contact is possible.

## 1.6 Scaffolds, roof brackets, hoists, and debris chutes [OSHA]
| Topic | Key rule |
|---|---|
| Scaffold fall protection | Required at **10 ft** (1926.451(g)). Don't confuse with the 6-ft roof rule |
| Scaffold capacity | Support own weight + **4×** max intended load (1926.451(a)(1)) |
| Platform planks | Extend over the support centerline **≥ 6 in**, and (for platforms ≤ 10 ft long) **≤ 12 in** unless cleated or restrained (1926.451(b)(4)-(5)) |
| Supported scaffold height-to-base > **4:1** | Must be tied, guyed, or braced (1926.451(c)(1)) |
| Competent person | Inspects scaffolds **before each shift** and after any occurrence that could affect integrity (1926.451(f)(3)) |
| Roof-bracket scaffolds | Fitted to the pitch; secured by nailing or anchorage, not relying on friction (1926.452(h)) |
| **Debris chutes** | Material dropped **more than 20 ft** outside the exterior walls must go through an **enclosed chute** (1926.252(a)) |
| Dropping through floor holes without chutes | Barricades **≥ 42 in high** and **≥ 6 ft** back from the projected edge of the opening above, with warning signs (1926.252(b)) |
| Material hoists | **No riding** unless designed for personnel (1926.552(b)). Rated load capacity posted |

## 1.7 Electrical hazards [OSHA]

### Plain English
Roofers are killed by **overhead power lines** (ladders, metal panels, conveyors, and hoists touching lines) and by **damaged cords and tools**.

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Work near energized parts | Don't permit work where contact is possible unless the circuit is **de-energized and grounded** or **guarded** (1926.416(a)(1)) |
| Clearance from overhead lines (unqualified workers / equipment) | Commonly **10 ft for lines up to 50 kV**, more for higher voltage (cranes/derricks: 1926.1408 Table A; general industry: 1910.333(c)(3), +4 in per 10 kV over 50 kV). **Treat every line as energized** until the utility confirms otherwise |
| GFCI | All **120-V, single-phase, 15- and 20-A** receptacle outlets on construction sites that are not part of the permanent wiring need **GFCI protection**, or an **assured equipment grounding conductor program** (1926.404(b)(1)) |
| Cords | Worn or frayed cords must not be used (1926.416(e)(1)). Use hard or extra-hard usage cords; no fastening with staples or hanging by nails |
| Tools | Double-insulated or grounded; don't remove the ground prong |

**Jobsite decision:** a crew is carrying a 30-ft metal ridge cap under a service drop. Best action: stop, reroute the carry, keep ≥ 10 ft, and call the utility to de-energize or insulate if clearance can't be maintained. "Wear rubber gloves" is **not** a substitute for clearance.

## 1.8 Power tools, pneumatic nailers, and material handling [OSHA / NIOSH]
- **Pneumatic tools:** secured to the hose by a positive means to prevent disconnection (1926.302(b)(1)). Compressed air may be used for cleaning only when reduced to **less than 30 psi**, and only with effective chip guarding and PPE (1926.302(b)(4)).
- **Nail guns (OSHA/NIOSH "Nail Gun Safety," 2011):** a **full sequential trigger** is the safest. **Contact (bump) triggers** roughly double the injury risk (double-fire and unintended fire). Other practices: never bypass the safety, disconnect air when clearing jams or moving, never point at people, and keep a hand away from the nail path.
- **Lifting / ergonomics [NRCA-P]:** use hoists or conveyors for bundles, lift with the legs, carry one bundle at a time, and use team lifts for rolls.
- **Conveyors / ladder hoists:** lockout, guards on pinch points, and nobody under the load.

## 1.9 Personal protective equipment and noise [OSHA]
| Hazard | Standard | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| General PPE | 1926.28, 1926.95 | Employer provides and pays for required PPE (with limited exceptions, e.g., ordinary safety-toe footwear) |
| Head | 1926.100 | Hard hats where there is a danger of head impact, falling objects, or electrical shock (ANSI Z89.1) |
| Eye/face | 1926.102 | Required for flying particles, molten materials, chemicals (ANSI Z87.1). **Hot asphalt → face shield + safety glasses** |
| Hearing | 1926.52 / 1926.101 | PEL **90 dBA for 8 h**; the time halves for every **5 dB** increase (95 dBA 4 h, 100 dBA 2 h, 105 dBA 1 h, 110 dBA 30 min, 115 dBA 15 min max). Above the table → controls plus a hearing conservation program |
| Respiratory | 1926.103 → 1910.134 | Written program, **medical evaluation before fit testing**, **fit test before first use and annually**, training |
| Hot work / asphalt | NRCA-P | Long sleeves, gloves with gauntlets, boots (not low shoes), pant legs outside boots so hot material can't run inside |

### Worked calculation: noise dose (1926.52 method)
A roofer runs a power roof cutter at 100 dBA for 1 h and works near a compressor at 95 dBA for 3 h. Permitted times: 100 dBA = 2 h, 95 dBA = 4 h.
Dose = C₁/T₁ + C₂/T₂ = 1/2 + 3/4 = 0.50 + 0.75 = **1.25**. That is **greater than 1.0**, so the exposure exceeds the PEL.

## 1.10 Hazard communication and hazardous materials

### Hazard Communication (1926.59 → 1910.1200, GHS-aligned) [OSHA]
- **SDS: 16 sections** in a fixed order. Examples: Sec. 2 Hazard ID, Sec. 4 First aid, Sec. 7 Handling/storage, Sec. 8 Exposure controls/PPE, Sec. 9 Physical/chemical properties (e.g., **flash point**).
- **Label elements:** product identifier, **signal word**, hazard statement(s), pictogram(s), precautionary statement(s), and name/address/phone of the manufacturer or importer.
- **Signal words:** **"Danger"** for the more severe hazards, **"Warning"** for the less severe.
- The employer needs a **written hazcom program**, a chemical inventory, SDSs **readily accessible** on every shift, and training before the initial assignment and when a new hazard is introduced.
- **[EDITION]** OSHA updated the HCS in 2024 (effective July 19, 2024, with phased compliance dates in 2026–2027). The core elements above are unchanged.

### Asbestos in roofing (1926.1101) [OSHA]. Reference R6 covers this (NOT READ)
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| PEL | **0.1 f/cc** 8-h TWA; excursion limit **1.0 f/cc** averaged over **30 min** |
| Class of work | Removal of asbestos-containing roofing is **Class II** work |
| Roofing-specific methods (1926.1101(g)(8)(ii)) | Remove **intact** to the extent feasible. Use **wet methods** where feasible and not a safety hazard. **Continuously mist** cutting machines. **Never drop or throw** material; lower it in a dust-tight chute, crane, or hoist, or bag or wrap it. Transfer to a closed receptacle. **Isolate or shut down roof-level HVAC air intakes** |
| PACM | Thermal system insulation and surfacing materials in buildings built **no later than 1980** are "presumed asbestos-containing" unless tested. Asphalt and vinyl flooring from that era is also treated as ACM unless shown otherwise. Old roofing felts, flashing cements, mastics, and asbestos-cement shingles are classic suspects: **test before tear-off.** |
| Supervision | A **competent person** for Class II work, with the required training |
| Environmental | EPA **NESHAP** (40 CFR 61 Subpart M) and **Illinois EPA** notification rules can apply to demolition/renovation. Nonfriable roofing that is sanded, ground, cut, or abraded can become regulated asbestos-containing material. **[JURISDICTION]** |

**Decision tested:** the best first step before tearing off a 1970s built-up roof with black flashing cement → **have suspect materials sampled and tested by a qualified inspector/lab.** Wetting and continuing is not the right first step, and neither is assuming it's fine because roofing asbestos is "nonfriable."

### Respirable crystalline silica (1926.1153) [OSHA]
- PEL **50 µg/m³** 8-h TWA; action level **25 µg/m³**.
- Roofing triggers: cutting **concrete/clay tile**, **fiber-cement** products, and masonry at chimneys and parapets.
- **Table 1** lists engineering controls (e.g., saws with integrated water delivery, or dust collection for fiber-cement saws) and the respirator required by task duration. Following Table 1 exactly exempts the employer from exposure assessment for that task.
- A **written exposure control plan** and a **competent person** are required. Specific Table 1 entries: **[verify in 1926.1153 Table 1]**.

### Lead (1926.62) [OSHA]
- PEL **50 µg/m³**; action level **30 µg/m³**.
- Relevant to old lead flashings, lead-painted surfaces, and cutting or soldering lead.

## 1.11 Hot work: asphalt kettles, torches, propane, and fire

### Hot asphalt [NRCA-P]
- **EVT (equiviscous temperature):** the temperature at which asphalt reaches the viscosity for proper application. Apply at **EVT ± 25°F** (mopping; mechanical spreader EVTs differ). Asphalt that is too cool gives poor adhesion and heavy moppings. Asphalt that is too hot drains off, loses properties, and increases fume and fire risk.
- **Flash point (FP):** never heat asphalt to or above its flash point. The common industry rule is to keep kettle temperature **at least 25°F below FP**; check the bill of lading or SDS **[MANUFACTURER]**.
- **Finished blowing temperature (FBT)**, listed on the bill of lading: do not hold asphalt above the FBT for extended periods.
- **Kettle safety:** a trained operator stays with the kettle. Keep lids closed (close them to smother a kettle fire; **never use water** on an asphalt fire). Keep the kettle level, away from building openings and air intakes, with an extinguisher nearby. Use a thermometer, and keep the kettle away from combustibles. **[JURISDICTION]** Fire codes (e.g., IFC §303) add separation distances and extinguisher ratings, and generally prohibit kettles on roofs.
- **Burn first aid [NRCA-P]:** cool with cool (not ice-cold) water. **Do not** try to peel asphalt off the skin or use solvents on it. Get medical care.

### Torch-applied roofing: NRCA CERTA program (public NRCA document)
- **Fire watch: minimum 2 hours after the last torch is turned off**, by a trained, dedicated watcher.
- **Never torch directly** onto a combustible deck, or at or into concealed spaces, flutes, gaps, or wood nailers where heat can enter. Use a **torch-safe** method (e.g., a self-adhered or mechanically fastened base, or heat-welding tools) at flashings and penetrations **[NRCA-P / CERTA]**.
- Keep **multiple 4A:60B:C** (or similar) extinguishers close to torching. Check the current CERTA manual for exact counts and distances **[EDITION]**.
- Propane cylinders: upright, secured, and away from torching and heat. Shut off cylinder valves when not in use. Check hoses and regulators for leaks with soapy water, **never a flame**.

### OSHA fire protection (1926.150–.153) [OSHA]
| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Building-area extinguishers | At least one **2A**-rated unit per **3,000 sq ft**, travel distance **≤ 100 ft** (1926.150(c)(1)(i)) |
| Flammable liquids/gas | At least one **10B** unit within **50 ft** of where more than 5 gal of flammable/combustible liquid or 5 lb of flammable gas is used (1926.150(c)(1)(iv)) |
| LP-gas | **No storage of LP-gas containers inside buildings** (1926.153(j)). Outdoor storage separation depends on quantity (e.g., 501–6,000 lb → 10 ft) |
| Flammable liquids | Approved containers; safety cans for ≤ 5 gal handling |

## 1.12 Heat, cold, first aid, recordkeeping, and inspections

### Heat stress [NRCA-P / OSHA guidance]
- Illnesses: **heat cramps → heat exhaustion** (heavy sweating, cool clammy skin, weakness) **→ heat stroke** (hot skin, confusion, possibly no sweating: **call 911**, cool aggressively).
- Controls: water, rest, shade, **acclimatization** (NIOSH: new workers ≤ 20% of a normal day's exposure on day 1, increasing gradually), buddy system, and early morning scheduling.
- **[EDITION/JURISDICTION]** OSHA proposed a federal heat standard in 2024. As of this writing I have **not verified** a final rule. The General Duty Clause applies either way.

### First aid (1926.50) [OSHA]
- Medical personnel must be available. In the absence of an infirmary, clinic, or hospital in near proximity, a person with a **valid first-aid certificate** must be on site.
- First-aid supplies must be accessible. Post emergency numbers.

### OSHA recordkeeping and reporting (29 CFR 1904) [OSHA]
| Event | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Work-related **fatality** | Report to OSHA within **8 hours** |
| In-patient **hospitalization**, **amputation**, or **loss of an eye** | Within **24 hours** |
| OSHA 300 log entry | Within **7 calendar days** of learning of a recordable case |
| **300A summary** | Posted **Feb 1 – Apr 30** |
| Retention | Records kept **5 years** |
| Small-employer exemption | ≤ **10 employees** at all times in the prior calendar year (roofing contractors are *not* in the partially exempt industry list) |

### OSHA inspection priorities and citations (general) [OSHA]
- Priorities: imminent danger → fatality/catastrophe → complaints/referrals → targeted → follow-up.
- Citation classes: **willful, serious, other-than-serious, repeat, failure to abate**. Maximum penalties are inflation-adjusted each January. **Don't memorize dollar figures** for this exam unless your reference gives them.
- Employees have the right to report hazards **without retaliation** (OSH Act §11(c)).
- **Illinois:** private roofers are under **federal OSHA (Region 5)**.

## 1.13 Quick comparison tables (easy to confuse)

### Thresholds
| Thing | Number |
|---|---|
| Roof / edge fall protection | 6 ft |
| Scaffold fall protection | 10 ft |
| Ladder/stair required | 19 in break |
| Ladder extension | 3 ft |
| Debris chute required | > 20 ft drop |
| Barricade for dropped debris | 42 in high, 6 ft back |
| Materials from roof edge (roofing) | 6 ft unless guardrail |
| Warning line | 6 ft (10 ft perpendicular to equipment) |
| Monitor-only roof width | ≤ 50 ft |

### Strength values
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| PFAS anchorage | 5,000 lb per worker |
| Positioning anchorage | 3,000 lb or 2× impact |
| SRL (≤ 2 ft free fall) | 3,000 lb |
| Guardrail top / mid / toeboard | 200 / 150 / 50 lb |
| Warning line stanchion / line | 16 lb / 500 lb |
| Covers | 2× imposed load |

> **WHAT TO MEMORIZE (Section 1 summary)**
> Everything in the two tables above, plus: 1,800 lb / 3.5 ft / 6 ft (PFAS) · 42 ± 3 in guardrail · 34–39 in warning line · 4:1 ladder · 4× ladder/scaffold load · 90 dBA/8 h, 5-dB exchange · GFCI 120 V 15/20 A · 10 ft from lines ≤ 50 kV · asbestos 0.1 f/cc & 1.0 f/cc/30 min · silica 50/25 µg/m³ · lead 50/30 µg/m³ · CERTA fire watch 2 h · fatality 8 h / hospitalization 24 h · 300A Feb 1–Apr 30 · 5-yr retention

> **HOW TO REASON THROUGH SAFETY ITEMS**
> - "Required" or "permitted" means OSHA text; "recommended" or "best practice" means NRCA.
> - When two answers are both legal, the BEST answer usually **eliminates** the hazard (de-energize, guardrail) rather than relying on behavior (monitor, "be careful").
> - Always ask: *what comes first?* Usually **stop the work / make it safe → inspect → then continue**.
