## 2. Nine skills a recall-only guide doesn't train

Each skill below shows a **sample stem style I wrote myself** to illustrate the skill. These are not CTS questions.

### 2.1 Picking the *best* answer among true statements
- **The trap:** three options are acceptable and one is *most* correct per the reference. Example of the pattern: several OSHA-permitted options for a low-slope roof, but only one fits the stated roof width or equipment.
- **How to win:** read the stem for **limiters**: "on a roof 50 ft or less in width," "when mechanical equipment is used," "NRCA recommends," "minimum," "maximum," "first," "most likely." The limiter decides the answer.

### 2.2 Sequencing roof work
- Examples: order of underlayment and drip edge at eave vs. rake. Order of base flashing, counterflashing, and cap flashing. Closed-cut valley: which plane's shingles go first. Tear-off, deck inspection, deck repair, dry-in. BUR: base sheet, plies, flood coat, aggregate.
- **How to win:** use the **water-shedding rule.** Every upper component laps *over* the component below it. Exceptions (for example, underlayment over the eave drip edge) are exactly what tests ask about. Memorize them.

### 2.3 Diagnosing installation errors (the "most likely cause")
- Examples: shingle blow-off → high nailing or unsealed strips. Blisters in BUR → trapped moisture or air between plies. Membrane shrinkage pulling at the perimeter. EPDM seam failure → dirty or unprimed laps. Wet insulation at a wall base → failed counterflashing.
- **How to win:** match the *symptom's location and timing* to the *mechanism*. A leak only during wind-driven rain points to flashing/laps. Ceiling stains in cold weather with no rain points to condensation or air leakage.

### 2.4 Material compatibility
- Examples: PVC vs. asphalt, EPDM vs. petroleum, polystyrene vs. hot asphalt and solvents, coal tar vs. asphalt, copper runoff onto galvanized or aluminum, and treated lumber vs. aluminum.
- **How to win:** keep a single "never touch" table in memory (Guide §2 and §4).

### 2.5 Safety *decisions*, not just safety *numbers*
- Examples: which fall-protection *system* is permitted in a given situation; what to do after a fall arrest; when a safety monitor alone is legal; what's wrong with an anchorage at foot level.
- **How to win:** know the *structure* of 29 CFR 1926.501(b) (which paragraph covers which situation), then the §1926.502 criteria.

### 2.6 Estimating calculations
- Examples: plan area → pitch factor → squares → waste → bundles. Ridge/hip linear feet. Felt rolls. Ventilation NFA. Asphalt pounds per square. Thermal movement. Markup vs. margin. Labor burden.
- **How to win:** write units on every line. Most wrong answers on calculation items are *unit* errors or *markup/margin* confusion.

### 2.7 Low-slope details
- Examples: base flashing height, cant strips (when and why), counterflashing lap, drain/sump detailing, pitch-pan limits, ponding definition, positive-drainage slope, seam laps and weld widths, wind zones (field/perimeter/corner).
- **How to win:** learn the NRCA *detail logic* (why each piece exists). Then numbers are easier to recall.

### 2.8 Commercial systems
- Examples: fully adhered vs. mechanically attached vs. ballasted. Insulation attachment. Cover boards. Vapor retarders (when and where). Steel deck attachment. Waterproofing (below-grade and plaza) vs. roofing. SPF and coatings.

### 2.9 Illinois-specific and business rules
- Illinois: limited vs. unlimited, the **8-units-or-less** line, bond **$10,000 / $25,000**, insurance **$250,000 PD / $500,000 BI** (Rule 1460.20), license number on **contracts, bids, ads, and commercial vehicles**, contract contents (**72-hour rescission**, deductible statement, trust statement in bold), qualifying-party rules (one QP, **30 business days**, **60 days**, **7 months**), fines.
- National business (NASCLA-type): FICA/FUTA, I-9, W-2/1099, record retention, lien basics, bonds, insurance types, balance sheet and income statement, ratios, markup vs. margin, OSHA 300 log.

## 3. Specific ways a generic AI study guide goes wrong (INFERENCE, based on common errors)

| Failure mode | Why it costs points |
|---|---|
| **Wrong blueprint weighting**, for example following the third-party table that prints 30/25 instead of 20/35 | Steep-slope under-studied, materials over-studied |
| **Code minimum presented as "the answer"** | The references are NRCA (best practice). When NRCA and a code minimum differ, pick the reference's figure. Code-only answers can be distractors. |
| **Invented precision.** Numbers stated confidently without a source, some wrong | A single wrong "memorize this" figure costs several items |
| **Obvious distractors** (e.g., "never use fall protection") | You learn to eliminate silly options, not to choose between close ones |
| **Illinois trivia overload** | The Act is maybe 5–10 items. The rest of Section 5 is business/law/estimating |
| **Residential bias** | 30 items are commercial/low-slope |
| **Pre-2025 Illinois numbers**, or post-2025 ones without the older values | Either way you can be caught out |
| **No "why."** Answer keys without reasoning | You can't transfer knowledge to a differently worded stem |

## 4. Answer-choice tactics for "BEST answer" items (INFERENCE, general test craft)
1. **Find the limiter word first** (minimum, maximum, first, most likely, NRCA-recommended, OSHA-required, Illinois).
2. **Classify the question's authority.** Is it asking OSHA law, NRCA practice, manufacturer instruction, Illinois law, or business math? Then answer from *that* authority.
3. **Eliminate "partially right, wrong scope."** For example, an OSHA rule that exists but applies to scaffolds, not roofs.
4. **Between two close options, prefer the one that protects water-shedding or life-safety *without exception*.**
5. **Absolute words** ("always," "never") are neither automatically wrong nor automatically right. OSHA and NRCA really do use "never" in places (never ride a material hoist; never heat asphalt above its flash point).
6. **Calculation items:** estimate the magnitude first so you can spot a unit error in the options.
7. **Time budget:** 150 min ÷ 130 ≈ 69 s per item. Do a first pass in ~100 minutes, flag hard items, and use the remaining ~50 minutes for flagged items and calculations. Never leave a blank. Unanswered items score as wrong (as reported by prep providers; CTS's scoring rules for blanks are not stated in S1).
