## 4.1 Low-slope basics

| Concept | What to know |
|---|---|
| **Waterproof vs. water-shedding** | Low-slope membranes are **waterproof**: continuous sheets with sealed seams. Steep-slope coverings **shed** water through laps |
| **Minimum slope** | **¼ in per ft (2%)** for BUR, mod-bit, single-ply, and SPF. **⅛ in per ft** for **coal-tar** BUR (IBC 1507) **[JURISDICTION]**. NRCA recommends designing for **¼:12** |
| **Ponding water** (NRCA definition) | Water that remains on the roof **48 hours after a rain**, under conditions that allow drying |
| **Positive drainage** | Slope by structure, tapered insulation, crickets between drains, and saddles behind curbs |
| **Secondary (overflow) drainage** | Required where parapets can trap water (overflow scuppers or secondary drains, per the plumbing and building codes) **[JURISDICTION]** |

### The system families
| Family | Material | How seams are made | Attachment options |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BUR (built-up roof)** | Alternating **plies** (felts) and **bitumen** (asphalt or coal tar), plus surfacing | Plies are shingled in hot bitumen or cold adhesive | Adhered in hot asphalt; base sheet mechanically fastened on nailable decks |
| **Modified bitumen (mod-bit)** | Asphalt modified with **SBS** (rubbery) or **APP** (plastic), with a polyester or glass reinforcement | Heat-fused laps, hot asphalt, cold adhesive, or self-adhered | Adhered, torched, self-adhered; base sheet fastened |
| **EPDM** | **Thermoset** synthetic rubber | **Adhesive tape** (seam tape + primer) | Ballasted, mechanically attached, fully adhered |
| **TPO** | **Thermoplastic** polyolefin | **Hot-air welded** | Mechanically attached, adhered, induction-welded plates |
| **PVC** | **Thermoplastic** polyvinyl chloride (plasticized) | **Hot-air welded** | Same as TPO |
| **SPF** | Spray polyurethane foam + elastomeric coating | Monolithic (no seams) | Self-adhered as sprayed |

**Thermoset vs. thermoplastic:** a **thermoset** (EPDM) is cured and **can't be re-melted**, so its seams need adhesive or tape. A **thermoplastic** (TPO, PVC) **softens when heated**, so seams are **welded**, and aged sheets can still be welded after cleaning.

## 4.2 Built-up roofing (BUR)

### Components, bottom to top
1. Deck (with a vapor retarder if required)
2. Insulation (plus cover board)
3. **Base sheet**
4. **Ply sheets** (commonly 3–4 plies of glass-fiber felts, ASTM D2178 Type IV/VI)
5. **Interply bitumen** between every ply
6. **Surfacing:**
   - **flood coat + aggregate** (gravel/slag);
   - **cap sheet** (mineral-surfaced);
   - **smooth surface + coating**.

### Application essentials [NRCA-P]
- **Shingle the plies:** each ply is lapped so the finished roof has the specified number of plies at every point. A 4-ply roof has 4 plies everywhere, including along the edges and laps.
- **No felt-to-felt contact.** Mop each ply fully and **broom it in** so it is completely embedded. Voids cause **blisters**.
- **Asphalt at EVT ± 25°F.** Too cool → poor adhesion and voids. Too hot → the asphalt degrades, its softening point drops, and it can **slip**.
- **Avoid phased application** (putting the base plies on one day and finishing weeks later). Moisture, dust, and dirt get trapped between the phases → blisters and delamination. NRCA discourages it.
- **Backnailing** plies to a nailer at the high edge prevents **slippage** on steeper low-slope roofs. The slope threshold depends on the edition/manufacturer **[EDITION]**; the concept is certain.
- **Aggregate:** embedded in a **flood coat** (≈ 60 lb/sq asphalt) at ≈ **400 lb/sq gravel** or **300 lb/sq slag**. It protects against UV, fire, and hail.
- **Cant strips** (45°) at wall and curb junctions. Bituminous plies and flashings **crack if bent 90°**.

### BUR defects: symptom → most likely cause
| Defect | What you see | Most likely cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Blister** | Raised, soft, domed area between plies | **Voids**, entrapped **air/moisture** between plies (poor mopping or embedment, wet felts, phased application) |
| **Ridging** | Long raised ridges, often over insulation joints | Movement at **insulation joints**, moisture condensation in joints, poorly secured insulation |
| **Splitting** | A straight tear through all plies | Thermal/structural **stress concentration** (at insulation joints or deck changes), inadequate reinforcement |
| **Slippage** | Plies sliding downslope, exposed laps at the top edge | **Wrong asphalt type** (softening point too low), **too much** interply asphalt, overheated asphalt, **no backnailing** |
| **Alligatoring** | Cracked "gator hide" surface | Aging and **UV exposure** of unprotected (smooth) asphalt |
| **Fishmouth** | Open wrinkle at a lap edge | Wrinkled felt, poor embedment at the lap |
| **Bare spots / loss of gravel** | Exposed felts | Wind scour, too little flood coat, gravel not embedded |

## 4.3 Modified bitumen

| Item | **SBS** (styrene-butadiene-styrene) | **APP** (atactic polypropylene) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Elastomeric** (rubbery); flexible in cold | **Plastomeric** (plastic); good heat and UV tolerance |
| Application | **Hot-mopped asphalt**, cold adhesive, **self-adhered**, or torch | **Torch-applied / heat-welded** (the classic), some cold-applied |
| Surfacing | Granules (cap sheet), or coated | Often smooth + coating, or granules |

- **Reinforcement:**
  - **Polyester** gives elongation and puncture resistance.
  - **Glass** gives dimensional stability.
- **Typical system:** a **2-ply** **base sheet + cap sheet**. Stagger the cap sheet's side laps from the base sheet's side laps.
- **Laps (typical) [MANUFACTURER]:**
  - **side laps ~3 in**, **end laps ~6 in**;
  - stagger end laps (e.g., **≥ 3 ft** apart);
  - cut a **diagonal "dog-ear"** at the end-lap corner where it meets the side lap.
- **Torching:** heat the roll and the substrate until the bitumen flows. A **uniform bleed-out of about ¼–½ in** at laps shows proper fusion **[MANUFACTURER]**.
  - No bleed-out → a cold, unfused lap.
  - Excessive bleed-out → overheating (and wasted modified asphalt).
- **Granules at end laps:** embed the granules (heat and press them in, or use a granule-free lap edge) before lapping, so the bitumen bonds to bitumen.
- **Cold weather:** warm the rolls (store them heated) to prevent cracking and help them relax.

**Fire:** torching is limited near combustible decks, concealed spaces, and flashings; follow NRCA's CERTA rules (see §1.11: **2-h fire watch**).

## 4.4 EPDM

| Item | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Nature | **Thermoset** rubber; common thicknesses **45 / 60 / 90 mil**; black (heat-absorbing) or white |
| **Seams** | **Seam tape + primer:** clean → **prime** both surfaces → let the primer **flash off** → apply tape → **roll with a silicone hand roller** toward the lap edge → cover T-joints and seam intersections with a patch **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| **Fully adhered** | Apply **bonding (contact) adhesive to both** the membrane underside and the substrate. Let it flash until **tacky but won't string**, then mate and broom/roll |
| **Ballasted** | Loose-laid under **~10–12 psf** of river rock or pavers. Limited to low slopes (commonly ≤ 2:12), buildings that can carry the weight, and moderate wind exposure **[MANUFACTURER/FM]** |
| **Mechanically attached** | Fasteners/plates in the laps or batten strips; enhanced in perimeters and corners |
| **Perimeter/penetration securement** | Required at walls, curbs, and penetrations (fastened strip or termination) so membrane tension or shrinkage can't pull at the flashings |
| **Relaxation** | Unroll and let the sheet **relax** (commonly ~30 min) before seaming or adhering, to remove fold wrinkles **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| **Flashing** | **Cured** EPDM for walls. **Uncured (semi-cured) EPDM** flashing for **irregular shapes** (inside/outside corners, pipes); it stretches and conforms |
| **Incompatible** | **Petroleum**, oils, asphalt, grease, animal fats (use PVC or walk pads near restaurant exhausts) |
| Repairs | Clean with the manufacturer's **weathered-membrane cleaner/primer**. **Round the patch corners.** Extend the patch beyond the damage (several inches per the manufacturer) |

**Common wrong answers:** "Weld EPDM seams with hot air." Thermosets can't be welded. "Adhesive on the membrane only." Contact adhesive goes on **both** surfaces.

## 4.5 TPO and PVC

| Item | TPO | PVC |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Thermoplastic polyolefin; **no liquid plasticizers** | Thermoplastic with **plasticizers** (or KEE-alloyed) |
| Seams | **Hot-air welded** | **Hot-air welded** |
| Strengths | Reflective (white), economical | **Chemical and grease resistance** (restaurants, industrial exhaust), weldability, long track record |
| Incompatibilities | Some solvents/oils; separation over old asphalt per the manufacturer | **Asphalt, coal tar, polystyrene** (plasticizer migration). Needs a **separation sheet** |

### Welding practice [MANUFACTURER / NRCA-P]
- **Test welds** at the start of each day and whenever conditions change (temperature, wind, humidity, a new power source). **Peel-test** the sample:
  - **Good weld:** the failure happens **in the membrane**, outside the weld (film tearing / scrim exposure), **not** at the weld interface.
  - **Cold weld:** too little heat or too fast; the seam peels apart cleanly.
  - **Burnt weld:** too much heat or too slow; the membrane is scorched or degraded.
- **Weld width:** the manufacturer's minimum is commonly **1½ in** for automatic welders (hand welds are often wider) **[MANUFACTURER]**.
- **Probe every seam** with a blunt probe (e.g., a cotter-pin puller) **after the weld cools**. Repair voids with a patch.
- **T-joints** in thicker membranes (where three layers meet) often need a **T-joint cover patch** **[MANUFACTURER]**.
- **Aged membrane:** clean with the manufacturer's weathered-membrane cleaner before welding repairs.
- **Cut edges of reinforced sheets** are sealed per the manufacturer (sealant/edge treatment for some products).

## 4.6 Flashings, terminations, and penetrations (NRCA detail logic)

| Detail | Key numbers / rules | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Base flashing height** | Minimum **8 in** above the finished roof surface **[NRCA-P]** (code minimums may differ) | Keeps water, snow, and splash below the top of the flashing |
| **Counterflashing** | Laps the base flashing about **4 in** (commonly); set into a **reglet** or surface-mounted with a termination bar and sealant | Sheds water over the top of the base flashing; lets the wall and roof move |
| **Termination bar** | Fastened near the top of the base flashing, sealed, and **covered by counterflashing** where possible | A termination bar alone with exposed sealant is maintenance-dependent |
| **Cant strip** | **45°**, wood fiber or perlite, at wall/curb junctions **for BUR and mod-bit** | Bituminous materials crack at 90° bends. **Not used with single-ply**; single-ply can bend 90° |
| **Curbs** | Minimum **8 in** above the roof surface **[NRCA-P]**; crickets on the upslope side of wide curbs | Same as base flashing |
| **Pipe penetrations** | **Prefabricated boots** (EPDM, TPO, PVC) or metal flanged collars stripped in | — |
| **Pitch pans (pitch pockets)** | **Last resort** (e.g., irregular structural shapes). Fill with **pourable sealer** crowned to shed water; **they require ongoing maintenance** | Sealant shrinks and pulls away. NRCA discourages them where a curb or boot will work |
| **Penetration spacing** | Leave room to flash (NRCA-style guidance: keep penetrations about **12 in** or more from walls, curbs, and each other) **[NRCA-P]** | You can't flash what you can't reach |
| **Roof drains** | Set in a **sump** (tapered insulation around the drain). Membrane/flashing sheet under the **clamping ring**, tightened evenly. BUR: a **lead or copper flashing sheet** (commonly ~30 × 30 in) set in mastic between the plies | The drain must be the lowest point, and the clamping ring must actually clamp |
| **Scuppers** | Through-wall box with flanges stripped in; **overflow scuppers** set higher than the primary drainage | — |
| **Roof edge / gravel stop** | Flange set in mastic (or on the membrane), fastened, and **stripped in** with plies or membrane. **ANSI/SPRI ES-1**-tested edge systems are required by the IBC for most low-slope membrane roofs **[JURISDICTION]** | Most wind failures start at the edge |
| **Wood nailers** | At perimeters and openings, **the same height as the insulation**, securely anchored to the structure (FM 1-49 gives values) **[FM/NRCA-P]** | They secure the edge metal and provide a base for fastening |
| **Expansion joints** | Raised curb-type joints with a flexible bellows at **building expansion joints**, deck direction changes, and dissimilar deck materials | Structural movement tears membranes |
| **Area dividers** | Low curbs that divide large roofs into sections (without being true expansion joints) **[NRCA-P]** | — |

### Wind zones
- **Corners** see the highest uplift, then **perimeters**, then the **field**.
- Enhanced attachment in corners and perimeters: more fasteners per board, closer seam or fastener spacing, half sheets.
- Design pressures come from **ASCE 7**. Tested assemblies are rated by **FM Approvals** (e.g., **Class 1-60, 1-90** = psf uplift ratings) or **UL 580/1897** **[JURISDICTION/FM]**.

## 4.7 Repair, maintenance, and leak/moisture diagnosis

### Maintenance [NRCA-P / Repair Manual scope]
- Inspect **at least twice a year** (spring and fall) and **after major storms**.
- Keep drains, scuppers, and gutters clear. Check flashings, counterflashings, sealants, seams, pitch pans, and walkways. Look for ponding and vegetation.
- **Temporary (emergency) repairs** stop water now: cold-applied mastic/tape compatible with the membrane. **Permanent repairs** follow later with matching materials.

### Repair methods by membrane (typical)
| Membrane | Repair |
|---|---|
| BUR | **Blister:** cut it out or X-cut it, dry it, re-adhere, and **patch with plies extending well beyond the cut** (commonly ~6 in). Remove gravel and prime before patching. Don't cut blisters that aren't leaking just because they exist; evaluate first |
| Mod-bit | Clean, heat or prime, patch with compatible mod-bit, and embed granules at the laps |
| EPDM | **Clean → prime → cured cover tape/patch** with rounded corners |
| TPO/PVC | **Clean (weathered-membrane cleaner) → weld a patch** of the same material, rounded corners, probe |

### Finding the leak
1. **Interview and map:** where it leaks inside, when (rain only? wind direction? snowmelt? cold dry days?), and since when.
2. **Look above and upslope** of the interior drip. Water travels along the deck, fasteners, and insulation joints before it shows.
3. **Check the usual suspects first:** flashings, counterflashings, penetrations, drains, seams, and roof edges.
4. **Water test:** start **low** and work **up**, flooding one component at a time, so you know which one leaked.
5. **Electronic leak detection:**
   - **Low-voltage EFVM** (electric field vector mapping) needs a **conductive** deck/surface path and a wet surface;
   - **high-voltage (spark) testing** works on a dry surface over a conductive substrate.
6. **Moisture survey** (IR, nuclear, capacitance) plus **core cuts** to find wet insulation that must be replaced (see §2.2).

### Leak or condensation?
| Clue | Points to |
|---|---|
| Wet only during/after rain, worse with wind from one direction | **Leak** (flashing, seam, penetration) |
| Wet on **cold days without rain**; frost or water on the underside of the deck; worse in winter over humid spaces | **Condensation** (vapor or **air leakage**; missing or failed vapor retarder or air seal) |
| Leaks when snow melts on sunny days | Ice/snow melt at a flashing, drain, or ice-dam equivalent (blocked drains or scuppers) |

## 4.8 Worked calculations

### A. Ballast load
A 100-square ballasted EPDM roof at **10 psf** of ballast:
- 100 sq × 100 sq ft/sq × 10 lb/sq ft = **100,000 lb = 50 tons**. The structure must be able to carry it **[verify with the structural engineer]**.

### B. Mechanically attached fastener count (field)
10-ft-wide sheets with **6-in** seam laps, fasteners at **12 in o.c.** in the laps.
1. Net coverage per sheet = 10 ft − 0.5 ft = **9.5 ft**
2. One fastener row per 9.5 ft of roof width, with a fastener every 1 ft along the row
3. Fasteners per sq ft = 1 ÷ (9.5 × 1) = 0.105 → **≈ 10.5 per square** in the field
4. For **80 field squares** → ≈ **842 fasteners** (perimeter and corner zones need more, e.g., half-width sheets double the rows)

### C. Drain sump / slope rise
With tapered insulation at **¼:12** and **30 ft** from the drain to the high point: rise = 30 × 0.25 = **7.5 in**.

### D. Mod-bit coverage with laps (rolls per square)
A cap roll is 39⅜ in × 32.8 ft (≈ 1 square gross) **[MANUFACTURER]**, with 3-in side laps and 6-in end laps.
- Net width = 39.375 − 3 = 36.375 in = 3.03 ft
- Net length = 32.8 − 0.5 = 32.3 ft
- Net ≈ 3.03 × 32.3 ≈ **98 sq ft** → **~1 roll per net square, plus waste**. That's why mod-bit rolls are marketed as "1 square" (gross) and estimators add waste.

> **WHAT TO MEMORIZE (Section 4)**
> Min slope **¼:12** (coal tar ⅛:12) · ponding = water remaining **48 h** after rain · thermoset EPDM = **tape/adhesive** seams; thermoplastic TPO/PVC = **hot-air welds** · adhesive on **both** surfaces (EPDM fully adhered) · ballast ~10–12 psf
> BUR: 3–4 plies shingled, **no felt-to-felt**, EVT ± 25°F, **no phased application**, flood 60 lb + gravel 400 / slag 300 lb per sq, **cant strips** for bituminous
> Defects: blister = voids/moisture between plies · slippage = wrong asphalt/too much/overheated/no backnailing · alligatoring = UV on bare asphalt · ridging = insulation joint movement
> SBS = elastomeric (mop/cold/self-adhered/torch); APP = plastomeric (torch) · mod-bit side lap ~3 in, end lap ~6 in, stagger ≥ 3 ft, **¼–½ in bleed-out**
> TPO/PVC: test welds daily and when conditions change · good peel = membrane tears outside the weld · probe cooled seams · weld width ~1½ in · PVC ✕ asphalt/coal tar/EPS · PVC best for grease
> Base flashing **≥ 8 in** · counterflashing laps ~4 in · curbs ≥ 8 in · pitch pans are a **last resort** · penetrations ~12 in apart · drain in a sump, clamping ring tight · corners > perimeters > field for uplift · **SPRI ES-1** edges

> **HOW TO REASON THROUGH LOW-SLOPE ITEMS**
> - Identify the **membrane family first** (thermoset, thermoplastic, bituminous). The seam method, flashing method, compatibility, and repair method all follow from it.
> - For a defect item, ask whether the cause is **bitumen chemistry** (type/temperature), **workmanship** (voids, laps, welds), **movement** (joints, deck), or **moisture** (trapped water, condensation).
> - For a detail item, the best answer keeps the top of the waterproofing **above water level** (8-in rule) and **covered** (counterflashing).
