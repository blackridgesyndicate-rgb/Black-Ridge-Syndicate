## Part A: Illinois Roofing Industry Licensing Act (225 ILCS 335) and Rules (68 Ill. Adm. Code 1460)

### A1. License types and scope [IL-V]
| Term | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Limited roofing license** | Roofing business limited to **residential properties of 8 units or less** (Act §2(h)) |
| **Unlimited roofing license** | Residential, **commercial, and industrial** (Act §2(i)) |
| **Roofing contractor** | Constructs, reconstructs, alters, maintains, and repairs roofs and "all kinds of roofing and **waterproofing** as related to roofing **over an occupiable space**" (§2(e)) |
| **License required** | Unlawful to engage in the business, act as, or hold out as a roofing contractor **or qualifying party** without a license or Department acceptance (§3(1)). Roofing/waterproofing work may be done only by a licensed/credentialed roofing contractor or qualifying party (§3(2)) |
| **Not required to be licensed** (Rule 1460.5) | Sheet metal, electrical, plumbing, refrigeration, or mason contractors, or carpenters, who do **chimney repairs** or install **soil pipes, electrical jacks, gutters, heating/cooling vents, wood roof decks** (or any other roof deck), or **TV antennas** |
| **Seller of roofing materials/services** | The qualifying-party rules don't apply when the work is performed by **someone other than the seller or the seller's employees** (§2(g), §3(5)) |

### A2. Getting and keeping a license [IL-V]
| Requirement | Detail | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Liability insurance | **$250,000 per occurrence property damage** and **$500,000 per occurrence personal injury/bodily harm**. The policy can't be cancelled without **30 days'** written notice to the Division | Act §3(2)(a); **Rule 1460.20** |
| Workers' comp | Coverage for roofing employees, or approved self-insurer | §3(2)(b) |
| Unemployment insurance | An IDES employer account number, and not delinquent | §3(2)(c) |
| **Surety bond** | **$10,000 limited / $25,000 unlimited**, continuous. The surety may terminate by filing a **60-day notice** with the Department (and notifying the contractor). **The license is cancelled without hearing on the bond termination date unless a new bond is filed** | §3(2)(d); **§3.2**; Rule 1460.30 |
| Exam | The qualifying party must pass before the license issues. **Failure to appear forfeits the exam fee** | §3.5 |
| Application window | **3 years** from the application date to complete, or it's denied and the fee is forfeited | §3(6) |
| Fees | Application **$125**; renewal **$62.50/year**; restoration **$50 + lapsed renewal fees (max $375)**; duplicate license **$20**; certification of record **$20** | Rule 1460.80 |
| Renewal cycle | Expires **December 31 of odd-numbered years** (2-year cycle) | Rule 1460.40 (verified secondary) |
| Restoration > 5 years lapsed | Add proof of active practice elsewhere, military service, or passing the appropriate exam (or **6 months** supervised practice under a same-type licensee) | Rule 1460.50(b) |
| Ongoing duty | Notify the Department in writing of **any change** in application information (e.g., assumed name) | §3(3) |
| Act repeal (sunset) date | **January 1, 2031** | Act headers **[EDITION]** |

### A3. Qualifying party (QP) rules [IL-V; EDITION: rewritten 8-15-2025]
- **Who:** the individual who takes the exam for the company. The QP is legally qualified to act for the business in all roofing matters, has authority to **supervise installation**, and is **actively engaged in day-to-day activities**.
- **Employment (§3(3.5)):** the QP must be an **employee**. The employer deducts **FICA and withholding** and provides **workers' comp**. **The QP may not receive a Form 1099** from the licensee.
- **One QP per licensee at all times** (§4.5(b)).
- **One licensee per QP (§4.5(a)).** Exception: the QP may serve **one additional** licensee **of the same license type** if there's **common ownership or management (≥ 25%)** or it's a licensed **subsidiary** (≥ 25% owned or managed).
- **When a QP leaves (§4.6):**
  1. **Both** the licensee and the QP notify the Department in writing (mail or email) within **30 business days** of the disassociation. Otherwise the license goes **inoperative**.
  2. The licensee names a **new QP within 60 days** after notifying the Department. Otherwise the license goes inoperative.
  3. The new QP must **take and pass the exam within 7 months** after Department approval. Otherwise the license is **automatically inoperative** until the company requalifies.
- **[EDITION]** The older Rule 1460.11(e), from 2021, says **30 business days** to name the new QP. The 2025 statute says **60 days**. **The statute controls.** If an exam item offers only 30 business days, it was likely written to the older rule.

### A4. License number display, advertising, vehicles [IL-V]
| Where | Rule | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **Contracts and bids** | License number **and** the licensee's name as it appears on the license (§5(a)) | Discipline |
| **Building permits** | The permit official affixes the license number to the application and permit (§5(a)) | — |
| **Office** | The license is displayed conspicuously at the principal office or place of business (§5(c)) | — |
| **Advertisements** | Must include the license number and name (§5(d)). The publisher doesn't have to verify it | **Knowingly** failing to display it, giving a false number, or letting another use your number is a **Class A misdemeanor with a $1,000 fine**. **Each day is a separate offense** (§5(e)) |
| **Commercial vehicles** | License number and name on **all commercial vehicles** used in offering services (§5.1) | **Civil penalty $250–$1,000**, enforceable by the Department, the **Attorney General**, or **local code enforcement** |

### A5. Required contract contents (§5.5) [IL-V; EDITION]
**Before starting any roofing work**, the contractor must provide a **written contract signed by both** the contractor (or designee) and the **property owner**. It must state at least:
1. the **scope** of services and **materials**;
2. the **approximate dates** of service;
3. for **repairs**, the **approximate costs** based on damage known when the contract is signed;
4. contractor contact information: **street address (not a P.O. box), email, phone**;
5. the **surety** and **liability insurer**, with the insurer's contact information;
6. the **cancellation and deposit-refund policy**, including a **rescission clause letting the owner rescind within 72 hours and get a full refund of any deposit**, plus a written statement that the owner may rescind;
7. a statement that if insurance proceeds will pay for the work, **the contractor cannot pay, waive, rebate, or promise to pay/waive/rebate any part of the insurance deductible**.

In addition:
- **On the face of the contract, in bold type:** the contractor will **hold in trust any payment** until **materials are delivered to the site** or a **majority of the work** is performed (§5.5(c)).
- Keep a fully executed copy **available for Department inspection** (§5.5(d)).
- **Public owners** (the State, municipalities, counties, school districts) must run a **bona fide procurement** in which the vendor or subcontractor holds verified active licenses and a QP credential (§5.5(e), new 2025).
- **[EDITION]** Before 8-15-2025 the Act required a **"land-based"** phone number. That word was removed.

### A6. Discipline and penalties [IL-V; EDITION]
| Item | Current law | Before 8-15-2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Discipline fines (§9.1) | Up to **$15,000 per violation** | $10,000 |
| Civil penalty for **unlicensed practice** (after a hearing) | Up to **$15,000 per offense** | $10,000 |
| Fine on a QP whose acceptance is suspended/revoked | Up to **$15,000 per violation** | $10,000 |
| Criminal: violating the Act | **Class A misdemeanor** (first offense), with a fine **not to exceed $2,500**. Second and subsequent offenses carry higher penalties (verify class in §9.x) | — |

**Selected grounds for discipline (§9.1(1)):**
- violating the Act or Rules;
- certain **criminal convictions** (felonies, or misdemeanors involving dishonesty or related to the profession);
- **fraud or misrepresentation** in getting or renewing a license;
- **professional incompetence or gross negligence**. Prima facie evidence includes a court judgment relating to roof construction or repair that **results in leakage within 90 days after completion**;
- **aiding or assisting** another to violate the Act;
- **failing to respond within 60 days** to a written Department request;
- **dishonorable, unethical, or unprofessional conduct** likely to deceive, defraud, or harm the public;
- substance abuse affecting practice;
- **discipline in another jurisdiction**.

> **HOW TO REASON: pre-2025 vs. current Illinois numbers**
> If only one of $10,000 / $15,000 appears, choose it. If both appear, the current statute says **$15,000**. I'd pick $15,000, but I can't know how CTS keyed an older item (INFERENCE). The **bond** ($10k/$25k), **insurance** ($250k/$500k), **8 units**, **72 hours**, **30 business days**, and **7 months** values did not change in 2025.

### A7. Related Illinois consumer law [NOT-CTS: know the headlines]
**Home Repair and Remodeling Act (815 ILCS 513):**
- **Written contract** required for residential jobs **over $1,000**. It must list the total cost, with parts and materials in reasonable detail (§15).
- The **"Home Repair: Know Your Consumer Rights" pamphlet** must be given. For jobs **over $1,000**, the customer signs the **Consumer Rights Acknowledgment Form** (§20).
- **Insurance-claim cancellation (§18(e)):** the homeowner may cancel before midnight on the **earlier of** the **5th business day** after written notice from the insurer that the claim isn't covered, **or** the **30th business day** after the insurer receives the proof of loss. Payments are refunded within **10 business days**.
- Contractors **may not advertise or promise to pay or rebate the deductible** (§18(b)). They must put the **roofing license number** on roofing contracts, bids, and ads (§18(d)).
- **FTC Cooling-Off Rule** (federal): home-solicitation sales carry a **3-business-day** right to cancel. The pamphlet repeats this.

## Part B: Business organization, employment, taxes (NASCLA-type topics)

### B1. Business entities
| Entity | Owner liability | Taxation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietorship** | **Unlimited personal** | Owner's personal return (Schedule C) + self-employment tax | Simplest. An assumed name is registered with the **county clerk** (Assumed Business Name Act) |
| **General partnership** | **Unlimited, joint and several** | Pass-through (Form 1065, K-1s) | A partnership agreement is strongly advised |
| **Limited partnership** | General partner unlimited; limited partners limited (if not managing) | Pass-through | — |
| **C corporation** | Limited | **Double taxation** (corporate tax, then dividends) | Board, officers, bylaws; files with the Secretary of State |
| **S corporation** | Limited | **Pass-through**; limited to **100 shareholders**, one class of stock, U.S. persons | An IRS election |
| **LLC** | Limited | Pass-through by default (can elect corporate) | Flexible. Files with the Secretary of State |

### B2. Hiring and payroll
| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Form I-9** | Employee completes Section 1 by the **first day of work**. Employer completes Section 2 within **3 business days**. Retain for **3 years after hire or 1 year after termination, whichever is later** |
| **Form W-4** | Employee withholding elections |
| **New-hire reporting** | Report to **IDES within 20 days** of hire (Illinois) |
| **FICA** | Social Security **6.2%** (employer) + 6.2% (employee) up to the annual wage base (adjusted yearly); Medicare **1.45% + 1.45%**, no cap (plus an additional 0.9% employee-only over $200k). **Employer total 7.65%** |
| **FUTA** | **6.0% on the first $7,000** per employee, with up to a **5.4% credit** for state UI paid → **net 0.6% = $42 per employee per year** (in a non-credit-reduction state) |
| **State UI (Illinois IDES)** | Experience-rated employer tax on wages up to the Illinois wage base |
| **Deposits/returns** | **Form 941** quarterly (withholding + FICA); **Form 940** annual (FUTA); **W-2s** to employees by **Jan 31**; **1099-NEC** to nonemployees by **Jan 31** |
| **1099-NEC threshold** | **$600** historically (NASCLA-era). **[EDITION]** Federal law enacted in 2025 raises it to **$2,000 for payments made after 2025**. Verify before relying on it |
| **FLSA** | Overtime at **1.5×** the regular rate for hours **over 40 in a workweek** (non-exempt employees). Payroll records kept **3 years**; time cards **2 years** |
| **Illinois minimum wage** | **$15.00/hour** (since Jan 1, 2025) |
| **IRS employment-tax records** | Keep at least **4 years** |
| **Illinois Employee Classification Act (820 ILCS 185)** [NOT-CTS] | In **construction**, a worker is **presumed an employee** unless the **ABC test** is met: free from control, work outside the usual course of the business, and an independently established trade |
| **Illinois Prevailing Wage Act** [NOT-CTS] | Public works pay prevailing wages; **certified payroll** is submitted to IDOL; records are kept (5 years) |

### B3. Contracts (general law)
- **Elements of a valid contract:**
  - **offer**;
  - **acceptance**;
  - **consideration**;
  - **legal capacity**;
  - **lawful purpose**.
  - Some contracts must also be **in writing** (statute of frauds, e.g., real-estate interests, contracts that can't be performed within a year, and sales of goods ≥ $500 under the UCC). Roofing statutes add their own writing requirements (above).
- **Contract types:**
  - **lump sum / fixed price**: contractor bears cost risk;
  - **cost-plus** (with or without a GMP);
  - **unit price**: e.g., per square or per sheet of deck replaced;
  - **time and materials**.
- **Key clauses:**
  - **scope/specifications**;
  - **change orders** (in writing, signed, **before** the work);
  - payment schedule and **retainage**;
  - **liquidated damages** (a pre-agreed daily amount for late completion);
  - **indemnification**;
  - **warranties**;
  - **differing site conditions**;
  - termination;
  - dispute resolution (**mediation → arbitration → litigation**);
  - **pay-when-paid** (timing) vs. **pay-if-paid** (condition precedent).
- **Breach:** remedies include damages, specific performance (rare in construction), and rescission. **Substantial completion** vs. **final completion** (the punch list).
- **Warranties:** **express** (written) vs. **implied** (workmanlike, habitability). **Manufacturer's** warranty (materials; sometimes **NDL**, "no dollar limit" system warranties) vs. **contractor's workmanship** warranty.

### B4. Liens, bonds, and insurance
- **Mechanics liens (Illinois Mechanics Lien Act, 770 ILCS 60)** [NOT-CTS; verify details]:
  - **Contractor:** record the lien **within 4 months after completion** to be effective against third parties (buyers, lenders), and sue to enforce within **2 years** after completion.
  - **Subcontractors:** serve notice on the owner **within 90 days after completing** their work. For owner-occupied residences there are shorter notices (60 days from first furnishing).
  - Before payment, the contractor gives the owner a **sworn statement** listing the subs and amounts, then collects **lien waivers**.
- **No liens on public property.** That is why public projects require **payment bonds**.
- **Surety bonds (three parties):** **principal** (contractor), **obligee** (owner), **surety** (bonding company). A surety is **not insurance** for the contractor; the contractor must reimburse the surety.
  - **Bid bond:** guarantees the bidder will sign the contract and furnish the other bonds (often **5–10%** of the bid).
  - **Performance bond:** protects the owner if the contractor defaults (usually **100%** of the contract).
  - **Payment bond:** protects **subs and suppliers** (usually 100%).
  - Federal **Miller Act** and the **Illinois Public Construction Bond Act** require them on public work above thresholds (verify the current thresholds).
  - **The Illinois license bond ($10k/$25k)** is a license/permit bond protecting the public and the State, not a performance bond.
- **Insurance:**
  - **CGL** (commercial general liability): **occurrence** vs. **claims-made**, with **completed-operations** coverage (injury or damage after the job is finished);
  - **commercial auto**;
  - **workers' compensation** (with employer's liability);
  - **umbrella/excess**;
  - **builder's risk** (the property under construction);
  - **inland marine** (tools and equipment).
  - Certificates: **certificate of insurance**, **additional insured**, **waiver of subrogation**.

## Part C: Financial management and estimating math

### C1. Financial statements
- **Balance sheet** (a snapshot on a date): **Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity**.
- **Income statement** (a period): **Revenue − Direct job costs = Gross profit; Gross profit − Overhead (indirect) = Net profit (before tax).**
- **Cash-flow statement:** operating, investing, and financing cash.
- **Ratios:**
  - **Current ratio** = current assets ÷ current liabilities (≈ 1.5–2.0 is usually considered healthy);
  - **Quick (acid-test) ratio** = (cash + receivables) ÷ current liabilities;
  - **Working capital** = CA − CL;
  - **Debt-to-equity** = total liabilities ÷ equity.
- **Accounting methods:**
  - **cash basis** vs. **accrual basis**;
  - for long contracts, **percentage-of-completion** vs. **completed-contract**;
  - **over-billing** (billed ahead of the work: a liability) vs. **under-billing** (work ahead of billing: an asset).
- **Depreciation (straight-line)** = (cost − salvage) ÷ useful life.

### C2. Markup vs. margin (the most-missed business calculation)
- **Markup** is a % of **cost**. **Margin** is a % of **selling price**.
- **Price = Cost × (1 + markup)**
- **Price = Cost ÷ (1 − margin)**
- margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup); markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin)

| Markup | Margin |
|---|---|
| 10% | 9.1% |
| 20% | 16.7% |
| **25%** | **20%** |
| 33.3% | 25% |
| **50%** | **33.3%** |
| 100% | 50% |

### Worked calculation 1: pricing a job with overhead and profit
The company expects **$240,000** annual overhead on **$1,200,000** of annual direct job costs.
1. Overhead rate = 240,000 ÷ 1,200,000 = **20% of direct cost**
2. A job has direct costs of **$18,000** (materials $8,000 + burdened labor $10,000). Overhead allocated = 0.20 × 18,000 = **$3,600**. Total cost = **$21,600**
3. The target is a **10% net profit margin** on the selling price: Price = 21,600 ÷ (1 − 0.10) = **$24,000**
4. Check: net profit = 24,000 − 21,600 = 2,400, and 2,400 ÷ 24,000 = **10%** ✔
   - Gross profit = 24,000 − 18,000 = 6,000 → gross margin **25%**
   - Markup on direct cost = 6,000 ÷ 18,000 = **33.3%**

**Trap:** adding 10% to cost (21,600 × 1.10 = $23,760) gives a **9.1% margin, not 10%**.

### Worked calculation 2: labor burden (illustrative rates; actual WC rates vary widely)
| Item | % of wages |
|---|---|
| FICA (employer) | 7.65% |
| FUTA (net, averaged) | ~0.6% |
| IL unemployment (example) | 3.5% |
| Workers' comp, roofing (example: $20 per $100 of payroll) | 20% |
| Liability insurance allocated (example) | 5% |
| Benefits (example) | 10% |
| **Total burden** | **46.75%** |

The base wage is $30.00/h, so the burdened rate = 30 × 1.4675 = **$44.03/h**.

### Worked calculation 3: labor cost from productivity
A crew of 4 working 8-h days installs **16 squares/day** → 32 labor-hours ÷ 16 sq = **2 labor-hours per square**.
- A 30-square job → 60 labor-hours × $44.03 = **$2,641.80** labor

### Worked calculation 4: break-even sales
Fixed overhead is $240,000/yr and the gross margin is 25%. Break-even revenue = 240,000 ÷ 0.25 = **$960,000**.

### Worked calculation 5: retainage
A $200,000 contract with **10% retainage**. The first progress bill is $50,000 → paid **$45,000**, retained **$5,000**. The retainage is released at completion or per the contract.

### Worked calculation 6: FICA on a paycheck
Gross wages of $1,000:
- Employer share = 62.00 + 14.50 = **$76.50**
- The employee has the same $76.50 withheld
- Total remitted = **$153.00**

### Worked calculation 7: current ratio
- Current assets: cash $80k + receivables $150k + inventory $70k = **$300k**
- Current liabilities = **$150k**
- **Current ratio = 2.0**
- **Working capital = $150k**
- **Quick ratio = (80 + 150) ÷ 150 = 1.53**

### Worked calculation 8: depreciation
A truck costs $60,000, has a $12,000 salvage value, and a 5-year life: (60,000 − 12,000) ÷ 5 = **$9,600 per year**.

> **WHAT TO MEMORIZE (Section 5)**
> Limited = **≤ 8 units residential** · bond **$10k / $25k**, surety termination **60-day** notice · insurance **$250k PD / $500k BI**, **30-day** cancellation notice · QP = W-2 employee (no 1099), **1 QP per license**, 2nd license only if same type + ≥ 25% common ownership/management or subsidiary · QP leaves → notify **30 business days** → new QP **60 days** → pass exam **7 months**
> License # + name on **contracts, bids, ads, commercial vehicles** · vehicle civil penalty **$250–$1,000** · ad violation Class A misdemeanor, **$1,000**, each day separate
> Contract: scope/materials, dates, repair cost estimate, street address/email/phone, surety & insurer, **72-h rescission w/ full deposit refund**, **no deductible rebate**, **bold trust statement** · fines **$15,000** (was $10,000) · leakage **within 90 days** = prima facie negligence · answer Department within **60 days**
> Fees: app $125, renewal $62.50/yr, restoration $50 + lapsed (max $375) · expires **Dec 31 odd years** · sunset **1/1/2031**
> FICA 7.65% · FUTA 6.0% on $7,000 less 5.4% credit = **$42** · I-9 Section 2 in **3 business days**; keep 3 yr after hire / 1 yr after termination (later) · OT > 40 h/wk · price = cost ÷ (1 − margin) · 25% markup = 20% margin

> **HOW TO REASON THROUGH SECTION 5 ITEMS**
> - For an Illinois item, ask: **license** (who and what), **money** (bond, insurance, fees, fines), **paper** (contract, ad, vehicle, permit), or **people** (qualifying party)?
> - For a business item, name the **document or statement** first (balance sheet, income statement, 941, I-9, lien, bond type). Then the answer usually follows.
> - For pricing math, write **"% of cost" or "% of price"** next to every percentage before calculating.
