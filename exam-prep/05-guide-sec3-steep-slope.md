# Study Guide — Section 3: Steep-Slope Roofing Systems (35 questions, 27% of exam: the largest section)

**CTS references most likely behind this section (NOT READ, paywalled):** *The NRCA Roofing Manual: Steep-slope Roof Systems* (CTS lists the 2021 edition; the current one is 2025), the *Architectural Metal Flashing and Condensation and Air Leakage Control* volume (ventilation), and the *Metal Panel* volume.

**What I used instead:** model-code text (IRC 2018/2021, flagged **[JURISDICTION]**, since Illinois has no statewide residential code), ASTM designations, manufacturer instructions (**[MANUFACTURER]**), and NRCA practice as widely published (**[NRCA-P]**).

**Exam logic (INFERENCE):** NRCA teaches that steep-slope roofs are **water-shedding**, not waterproof. Nearly every detail question comes down to one idea: **the upper piece laps over the lower piece, so water never runs into a lap.**

## 3.1 Asphalt shingles: products and ratings

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **3-tab (strip)** | Single-layer shingle, 12 × 36 in, 5-in exposure, 3 tabs separated by cutouts |
| **Laminated (architectural, dimensional)** | Two or more bonded layers; metric size ~13¼ × 39⅜ in, 5⅝-in exposure. Heavier and more wind-resistant; **no cutouts to align** |
| **Glass-fiber mat** | ASTM **D3462** (the modern standard). Organic-mat shingles are obsolete |
| **Wind resistance** | ASTM **D7158** Classes **D / G / H** (~90 / 120 / 150 mph). ASTM **D3161** Classes **A / D / F** (~60 / 90 / 110 mph) |
| **Impact** | UL **2218** Classes **1–4** (Class 4 is the most impact-resistant) |
| **Fire** | ASTM **E108 / UL 790** Classes **A** (best), B, C |
| **Sealant strip** | Factory adhesive that bonds each course to the one below after sun heats it |
| **Starter strip** | The first course at the eave (and often the rakes), with sealant near the edge, so the first exposed course is sealed and its cutouts are backed |

### Minimum slope and underlayment [JURISDICTION: IRC R905.2.2 / R905.1.1; NRCA-P agrees]
| Slope | Asphalt shingles allowed? | Underlayment |
|---|---|---|
| **< 2:12** | **No** | Use a low-slope membrane system |
| **2:12 to < 4:12** ("low-slope asphalt shingle application") | Yes | **Double coverage.** Start with a **19-in** strip at the eave, then **36-in** sheets lapped **19 in** (each point has two layers), or a self-adhered membrane per the manufacturer |
| **≥ 4:12** | Yes | **Single layer**, lapped shingle-fashion with **2-in** head laps and end laps offset (end-lap dimension per code/manufacturer, commonly 4–6 in) |

### Ice barrier (IRC R905.1.2) [JURISDICTION]
- Where local practice or the building official requires it ("history of ice forming at the eaves").
- Use **two layers of underlayment cemented together** or a **self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen sheet** (ASTM D1970).
- It runs from the **lowest edge of all roof surfaces** to **at least 24 in inside the exterior wall line**. See the worked calculation in Guide §2.10-E.
- Northern Illinois practice nearly always includes an ice barrier. It is also common practice at **valleys** and around **penetrations** **[NRCA-P/MANUFACTURER]**.

## 3.2 Sequencing at the eave and rake: the most-tested installation order

### Drip edge and underlayment [JURISDICTION: IRC R905.2.8.5]
| Edge | Order (bottom → top) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Eave** | Deck → **drip edge** → underlayment/ice barrier **over** the drip edge flange → starter → shingles | Water running down the underlayment lands **on top of** the metal and drips off. It never gets behind the flange |
| **Rake** | Deck → underlayment → **drip edge over** the underlayment → shingles | Wind-driven water can't get under the underlayment edge at the rake |
| Drip edge laps & fastening | Lap joints **≥ 2 in**. The flange extends **≥ 2 in** back onto the roof. Fastened **≤ 12 in o.c.** | — |

**Watch out [MANUFACTURER]:** some ice-barrier manufacturers let their membrane go directly on the deck at the eave with the drip edge on top. If a question says **"per code"** or **"standard sequence,"** use the table above. If it says **"per the manufacturer,"** the stem will tell you.

### Starter course and overhang [MANUFACTURER]
- Starter goes along the eaves (and rakes, for wind resistance), with the **sealant near the edge**.
- Shingles typically overhang the drip edge about **¼–¾ in** (per the manufacturer).
- **Common error:** a 3-tab laid upside-down as a starter puts the sealant at the wrong edge and leaves the cutouts exposed. Use a purpose-made starter or a 3-tab with the tabs cut off.

### Overall sequence for a tear-off reroof [NRCA-P]
1. Tear off in sections you can dry-in the same day.
2. Inspect the deck; replace rotten/delaminated sheathing and re-nail.
3. Eave drip edge.
4. Ice barrier.
5. Underlayment.
6. Rake drip edge.
7. Valley linings.
8. Starter.
9. Field shingles, with step flashing woven in course-by-course.
10. Counterflashing and vents.
11. Ridge/hip caps.
12. Clean up and do a magnet sweep.

## 3.3 Fastening asphalt shingles

| Rule | Value / reason |
|---|---|
| Number | Min. **4 fasteners per strip shingle** (2 per individual shingle) [IRC R905.2.6]. **6** for high-wind areas, mansards, and slopes **> 21:12**, plus **hand-sealing** on very steep slopes **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| Fastener | 12-ga shank, ⅜-in head, corrosion-resistant. Penetrates **¾ in into or through** the deck (see §2.8) |
| Location | In the manufacturer's **nailing zone**, typically just **below the sealant line**, so each nail passes through the **underlying course** too (double coverage) |
| **High nailing** (above the zone) | Nail misses the course below → **blow-offs** and slippage. **The #1 "most likely cause" of shingle blow-off** along with unsealed strips |
| **Overdriven** | Cuts the mat; loses holding power |
| **Underdriven** | Nail head props the shingle up → prevents sealing, **nail pops**, tears the shingle above |
| **Crooked/angled** | The edge of the head cuts the shingle |
| Staples | Not recommended by NRCA; many manufacturers void wind warranties **[MANUFACTURER]** |

**Cold-weather installation:** the sealant strip won't activate until warm, sunny weather. For immediate wind resistance, **hand-seal** with dabs of asphalt cement per the manufacturer.

## 3.4 Layout, offsets, and appearance problems
- **3-tab:** the common offset is **6 in** (cutouts not aligned; they repeat every other course).
- **Laminated:** random or a minimum offset **per the manufacturer** (often ≥ 4–6 in).
- **"Racking" (straight-up) installation:** stepping shingles straight up the roof. Many manufacturers **prohibit or discourage it**, because it shows color shading and puts fastener lines on top of each other. The recommended method is **diagonal (stair-step) application** **[MANUFACTURER]**.
- **Shading / color variation:** mixing **production lots** or racking. **Use bundles from the same lot** and blend from several bundles.
- **Chalk lines:** snap lines to keep courses straight. Check exposure every few courses.

## 3.5 Valleys

| Valley | How | Good for | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open, metal-lined** | Metal (W-profile/center crimp) over underlayment. Shingles are trimmed back from the centerline, and the exposed width widens toward the eave (commonly **~⅛ in per ft**) | Heavy water, all shingle types; long life | No nails through the metal center. Hold the metal with **cleats/clips** so it can move. Min metal width per code (IRC Table R905.2.8.2: **24 in** wide for metal) **[JURISDICTION]** |
| **Open, mineral-surfaced roll roofing** | **18-in** bottom layer (granules down), then a **36-in** top layer (granules up) | Economy | Shorter life |
| **Woven** | Shingles from both planes woven across the valley | **3-tab** shingles | **Not recommended for laminated shingles** (too stiff/thick) **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| **Closed-cut** | Shingles from **one plane** run across the valley ≥ **12 in** onto the other plane. Shingles from the **other** plane are **cut ~2 in back** from the centerline; clip the upper corner at 45°; embed the cut edge in a band of asphalt cement | Laminated and 3-tab | **No fasteners within ~6 in of the centerline** **[MANUFACTURER]** |

**Closed-cut: which plane goes first?** Most manufacturers say: run the shingles from the plane with the **lower slope or less water (smaller area) across the valley first.** Then cut the shingles on the **steeper or larger** plane, whose faster water flows over the cut edge **[MANUFACTURER]**.

**Valley underlayment:** a **36-in** wide ice barrier or underlayment centered in the valley, installed **before** the field underlayment laps over it **[NRCA-P/MANUFACTURER]**.

## 3.6 Flashing: walls, chimneys, penetrations

### Step flashing (sidewall)
- **L-shaped pieces**, one per shingle course, woven into the courses.
- **Size:** min **4 × 4 in** by code (IRC R905.2.8.3). NRCA/manufacturer practice uses pieces about **2 in longer than the exposure** (e.g., **~7 in long for a 5-in exposure**) with legs ~4–5 in.
- Each piece laps the one below by **≥ 2 in**.
- **Nail only the roof leg, near the top**, into the deck. **Never nail the wall leg or through both legs.** That lets the roof and wall move independently.
- **Counterflashing** (siding, a separate counterflashing, or the wall's weather-resistive barrier and trim) covers the vertical legs. On lapped siding, keep the siding clear of the roof surface per the manufacturer (often **1–2 in**).
- **Continuous "L" flashing instead of steps is wrong on a shingle sidewall.** Water running down the vertical leg enters the laps.

### Kickout (diverter) flashing [JURISDICTION: IRC R903.2.1]
- Required where the roof-wall intersection ends **before the wall does** (the eave meets a wall that continues).
- It diverts water into the gutter instead of behind the siding.
- A **missing kickout** is a classic cause of **rotted wall sheathing below a roof-wall intersection**.

### Headwall (front/apron) flashing
- Where the roof slopes **up to** a wall.
- Continuous apron flashing: min **4 in** up the wall and **4 in** onto the shingles (practice varies).
- Counterflashed by the wall cladding.

### Chimney flashing
- **Components:**
  - **Apron** on the downslope face.
  - **Steps** on the sides.
  - **Back (upslope) flashing or cricket.**
  - **Counterflashing** set into the **mortar joints** (a reglet cut about 1–1½ in deep and sealed), lapping the base/step flashing.
- **Cricket (saddle):** required on the upslope side of any chimney or penetration **more than 30 in wide** measured perpendicular to the slope (IRC R903.2.2 / R1003.20) **[JURISDICTION]**. It diverts water around the chimney.
- **Why two pieces (base + counter)?** The chimney and the roof move independently. The base flashing is fastened to the roof; the counterflashing is fastened to the masonry and simply laps over it.

### Pipe/vent flashings
- The flange's **upper portion goes under** the shingles above, and the **lower portion goes over** the shingles below.
- Boots/collars are sized to the pipe. Lead or neoprene boots.
- **Plastic or rubber collars crack from UV.** That is a common leak source on 10–15-year-old roofs.

### Skylights
- Use the **manufacturer's flashing kit** (step or continuous side flashings, head and sill flashings). The same laps and sequencing logic applies.

## 3.7 Hips and ridges
- **Hip and ridge shingles** (manufactured, or cut 3-tab pieces 12 × 12 in) at ~**5-in exposure**.
- Hips are capped from the **bottom (eave) up**.
- **Ridge courses:** a common practice is to start at the end **opposite the prevailing wind** so the laps face away from it **[NRCA-P/MANUFACTURER]**.
- **Ridge vent:** the deck is cut back per the vent manufacturer (typically about **1 in each side** of the ridge, or 1¾ in at a ridge board). The vent is covered with cap shingles using **longer nails** to reach the deck.

---

## 3.8 Reroofing over existing asphalt shingles [JURISDICTION / NRCA-P]
- **Allowed** by the model codes if there is only **one existing layer** and it is not wet or deteriorated (see §2.2).
- The existing roof must be **flattened** (curled shingles cut, and so on).
- Replace **flashings** as needed; drip edge is often added.
- Use **longer nails** to penetrate the deck ¾ in.
- The **"nesting"** technique is used for 3-tab over 3-tab: new courses butt against the old course lines.
- NRCA **prefers tear-off** (deck inspection, less weight, better performance).

## 3.9 Diagnosing asphalt shingle problems

| Symptom | Most likely cause(s) | Not the best answer |
|---|---|---|
| Shingles **blown off** along lines | **High nailing**, unsealed sealant strips (cold install, dust), too few nails | "Wind was too strong." Rated shingles resist their rated wind when installed correctly |
| **Curling/cupping** | Age; **inadequate attic ventilation** (heat/moisture from below); moisture | Poor alignment |
| Shingles **buckling/wavy lines** | **Wet or wrinkled underlayment**, deck movement (no H-clips or panel gaps), deck moisture | UV |
| **Nail pops** | **Underdriven** nails, deck movement or shrinkage | Too many nails |
| **Granule loss** in spots | Hail, foot traffic, blistering, age; downspout discharge from an upper roof | — |
| **Black streaks** | **Algae** (*Gloeocapsa magma*). Cosmetic. **Copper/zinc** strips or algae-resistant granules help | Mold rot or failure |
| **Leak at the base of a sidewall** | **Missing kickout**, reversed step laps, continuous L-flashing | Shingle defect |
| **Leak along the valley** | **Nails too close to the centerline**, a missing valley underlayment, cut edges not sealed | — |
| **Ice dams / eave leaks in winter** | **Heat loss** into the attic (air leaks, low insulation) plus **poor ventilation**. Snow melts and refreezes at the cold eave | "Needs thicker shingles." **An ice barrier limits damage; air sealing, insulation, and ventilation prevent the dam** |
| **Color shading patches** | Mixed **production lots**, racking | Weathering |

## 3.10 Clay and concrete tile

| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Min slope | **2½:12** (IRC R905.3.2). **2½:12 to < 4:12 requires double underlayment** [JURISDICTION] |
| Types | Clay: mission/barrel (pan and cover), S-tile, flat. Concrete: flat or profiled |
| **Weight** | Heavy: roughly **9–12 psf** for many concrete tiles (lighter-weight products exist) **[MANUFACTURER]**. **The structure must be checked** before converting from asphalt |
| Underlayment | **The underlayment is the real waterproofing layer.** Tile sheds most water, and the underlayment handles what gets past. Use durable (often high-temp) underlayment; it may be replaced before the tile wears out |
| Attachment | Nails (corrosion-resistant), **battens** (with counter-battens or spacers for drainage), wind clips, **foam adhesive**, or wire-tie systems, **per the wind zone** **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| Headlap | Commonly **≥ 3 in** **[MANUFACTURER]** |
| Eave | **Bird stop / eave closure** and a **raised fascia/eave riser** so the first course lies at the correct angle |
| Hips & ridges | On a **ridge/hip nailer board**, with mortar or a dry-set ventilated ridge system |
| Walking | Step on the **lower (headlap) portion** of the tile, near the nose of the course below, or use walk boards. Never on unsupported edges |
| Cutting | **Silica** hazard (see §1.10). Use wet cutting or dust collection |
| Valleys & walls | Metal valleys and pan flashings (e.g., "channel" or pan flashing at sidewalls) sized for tile |

## 3.11 Slate

| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Min slope | **4:12** (IRC R905.6.2) **[JURISDICTION]** |
| Headlap | The standard is **3 in** (NRCA practice varies with slope: more headlap on lower slopes, e.g., 4 in near 4:12, and less on very steep slopes) **[EDITION/NRCA-P]** |
| **Exposure** | **(slate length − headlap) ÷ 2** |
| Fastening | **Two** nails per slate, **copper or stainless**. **Don't drive nails tight.** The slate should **"hang"** on the nails, or it cracks |
| Grades | ASTM **C406**: **S1** (> 75 yr service), **S2** (40–75), **S3** (20–40) |
| Tools | **Slate ripper** (removes broken slates by cutting nails), slater's hammer, slate cutter, stake |
| Repair | Slide in a replacement and secure it with a **slate hook**, or use the **nail-and-bib method** (nail in the joint above, covered with a copper bib) |
| Walking | Don't walk on slate. Use roof ladders, hooks, or scaffolding |
| Flashings | Long-life metals: **copper**, stainless |

### Worked calculation: slate exposure and count
- **Exposure:** a 20-in slate with a 3-in headlap → (20 − 3) ÷ 2 = **8.5 in**.
- **Slates per square** (10-in-wide slates): the exposed area of one slate = 8.5 × 10 = 85 sq in. 14,400 ÷ 85 = **169.4 → 170 slates** (plus waste).

## 3.12 Wood shingles and shakes [JURISDICTION: IRC R905.7 / R905.8]

| Topic | Wood shingles (sawn) | Wood shakes (split) |
|---|---|---|
| Min slope | **3:12** | **3:12** |
| Interlayment | Not required | **18-in-wide No. 30 felt interlayment** between each course (the bottom edge above the butt line at a distance of **2× the exposure**) |
| Spacing between pieces | **¼–⅜ in** | **⅜–⅝ in** |
| Side-joint offset | **≥ 1½ in** course to course | **≥ 1½ in** |
| Fasteners | 2 per piece, **¾ in from the edges**, and not more than ~**1 in above the exposure line** of the next course. **Stainless steel or hot-dip galvanized** | Same |
| Sheathing | **Spaced** or solid boards | **Spaced** or solid |
| Starter | **Doubled (or tripled)** at the eave | Doubled |

### Wood shingle maximum exposure (common IRC values; check your code edition) [JURISDICTION]
| Length | 3:12 to < 4:12 | ≥ 4:12 |
|---|---|---|
| 16 in (No. 1) | 3¾ in | **5 in** |
| 18 in (No. 1) | 4¼ in | **5½ in** |
| 24 in (No. 1) | 5¾ in | **7½ in** |

**Pattern:** on steeper roofs the exposure can be longer. At 4:12 and up, the exposure is less than one-third of the shingle length, which keeps about **three layers** at every point.

## 3.13 Metal steep-slope roofing

### System types
| System | Description | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Standing seam, concealed clip** | Panels joined at raised seams; concealed clips attach the panel to the deck/purlins | **Clips allow thermal movement** (fixed vs. sliding/expansion clips). **Structural** (spans purlins) vs. **architectural** (needs a solid deck and underlayment) |
| **Exposed-fastener (through-fastened)** | Panels screwed through the face | Screws with **EPDM washers**, seated snug (not overdriven or cocked). Thermal movement **wallows out holes** over time → leaks |
| **Metal shingles/shakes** | Interlocking pressed units | Min **3:12** (IRC R905.4.2) |

### Minimum slopes (IRC R905.10.2) [JURISDICTION]
| Panel | Min slope |
|---|---|
| Standing seam | **¼:12** |
| Lapped, non-soldered seams **with applied lap sealant** | **½:12** |
| Lapped, non-soldered seams **without** lap sealant | **3:12** |

### Materials and coatings
- **Galvanized steel** (e.g., **G90** zinc coating).
- **Galvalume** (aluminum-zinc, e.g., **AZ50/AZ55**). Do not contact wet concrete, mortar, or lead.
- **Aluminum.**
- **Copper**, specified by weight in **ounces per sq ft** (e.g., **16 oz, 20 oz**).
- **Zinc**, **stainless**, and **terne** (coated steel).
- **Paint finishes:** **PVDF ("Kynar 500"/Hylar, 70% resin)** has the best color and chalk retention. **SMP** (silicone-modified polyester) is lower cost.

### Handling and installation [MANUFACTURER/NRCA-P]
- **Cut with shears, nibblers, or a panel saw. Do not use abrasive (friction) blades.** Hot particles burn the coating, embed in the panel, and rust.
- **Remove metal shavings** (swarf) the same day. They rust-stain the finish.
- **Oil canning:** visible waviness in flat areas. Mostly cosmetic. Reduce it with a heavier gauge, striations or pencil ribs, a flat substrate, and correct clip installation (not over-tightened).
- **Underlayment:** a high-temperature self-adhered or synthetic product rated for metal.
- **Snow guards** protect people, gutters, and lower roofs from sliding snow.
- **Dissimilar metals:** see §2.8.

### Thermal movement (ΔL = α × L × ΔT)
| Metal | α (in/in/°F, approx.) |
|---|---|
| Steel | **6.5 × 10⁻⁶** |
| Copper | **9.4 × 10⁻⁶** |
| Aluminum | **12.8 × 10⁻⁶** |
| Zinc | ~**12–17 × 10⁻⁶** (depends on grain direction/alloy) |

### Worked calculation: panel expansion
A 40-ft panel sees a 150°F surface-temperature swing (−10°F winter night to 140°F summer surface).
1. L = 40 ft × 12 = **480 in**
2. **Steel:** 6.5 × 10⁻⁶ × 480 × 150 = **0.468 in** (≈ ½ in)
3. **Aluminum:** 12.8 × 10⁻⁶ × 480 × 150 = **0.922 in** (≈ 1 in)

**Aluminum moves about twice as much as steel.** That's why long panel runs need **sliding clips** and a fixed point, and why exposed-fastener panels on long runs leak at the screws.

## 3.14 Attic ventilation (steep-slope residential)

### Why ventilate [NRCA-P / IRC]
- Removes **moisture** (preventing condensation, mold, and wet sheathing).
- Reduces **summer heat** (longer shingle life, lower cooling load).
- Reduces **ice dams** (keeps the roof deck cold in winter, along with air sealing and insulation).
- Shingle warranties often require code-compliant ventilation **[MANUFACTURER]**.

### Code minimum (IRC R806.2) [JURISDICTION]
- The net free ventilating area (**NFA**) must be at least **1/150** of the vented attic floor area.
- It may be reduced to **1/300** when **either** of these is met:
  1. In **Climate Zones 6, 7, 8**, a **Class I or II vapor retarder** is installed on the warm-in-winter side of the ceiling. (Illinois is **CZ 4–5**, so this route rarely applies here.)
  2. **At least 40% and not more than 50%** of the required area is in the **upper portion** (exhaust, within **3 ft of the ridge**), and the rest is in the **eave/soffit** (intake).

### Worked calculation: ventilation
The attic floor is 1,800 sq ft, using the 1/300 option with a balanced intake/exhaust split.
1. Required NFA = 1,800 ÷ 300 = **6 sq ft** = 6 × 144 = **864 sq in**
2. 50% exhaust = **432 sq in**; 50% intake = **432 sq in**
3. A ridge vent rated **18 sq in NFA per linear foot** **[MANUFACTURER]** → 432 ÷ 18 = **24 LF** of ridge vent
4. Using 1/150 instead: 1,800 ÷ 150 = 12 sq ft = **1,728 sq in**

### Ventilation rules of thumb (the "best answer" logic)
- **Balanced:** intake **≥** exhaust. Many manufacturers want intake ≥ exhaust so the exhaust vents don't pull conditioned air or weather in **[MANUFACTURER]**.
- **Don't mix exhaust types** in the same attic (e.g., ridge vent plus gable louvers or a power fan). The second exhaust **short-circuits** the flow, and the ridge vent can pull rain and snow in through the other exhaust.
- **Baffles (vent chutes)** keep insulation off the soffit vents and maintain an air channel (IRC R806.3 requires at least **1 in** of air space between the insulation and the sheathing at the vent).
- **Bath and kitchen fans** must exhaust **to the outdoors**, never into the attic.
- **Net free area** is the actual open area. It's always less than the vent's gross size (screens and louvers block part of it).
- **Unvented (conditioned) attics** are allowed under specific rules (IRC R806.5): air-impermeable insulation at the roof deck, or equivalent **[JURISDICTION]**.

> **WHAT TO MEMORIZE (Section 3)**
> Shingles min **2:12**; 2:12–<4:12 **double underlayment (19-in starter, 19-in laps)**; ≥ 4:12 single layer, 2-in head lap · ice barrier to **24 in inside exterior wall line** · **eave: drip edge under underlayment; rake: drip edge over underlayment** · drip edge laps 2 in, ≤ 12 in o.c.
> 4 nails (6 for high wind / > 21:12) · 12 ga, ⅜ head, ¾ in into deck · **high nailing = blow-offs** · underdriven = nail pops · hand-seal in cold weather
> Valleys: metal ≥ 24 in; roll roofing 18 + 36 in; woven ≠ laminated; closed-cut: lower-slope/smaller plane first, cut 2 in from centerline, clip corner, no nails within 6 in
> Step flashing ≥ 4×4 in, one per course, nail roof leg only · **kickout** at wall-eave · **cricket > 30 in** wide chimney · counterflashing in mortar joints
> Tile **2½:12** (double underlayment to 4:12) · slate **4:12**, exposure = (L − headlap)/2, copper/stainless, hang not tight, S1 > 75 yr · wood shingles/shakes **3:12**; shakes need 18-in No. 30 interlayment; spacing ¼–⅜ (shingles) / ⅜–⅝ (shakes); offsets ≥ 1½ in
> Metal: standing seam **¼:12**, lapped + sealant **½:12**, lapped no sealant **3:12**, metal shingles **3:12** · α steel 6.5, copper 9.4, aluminum 12.8 (× 10⁻⁶/°F) · no abrasive cutting
> Ventilation **1/150**, or **1/300** with 40–50% high exhaust (within 3 ft of ridge) · don't mix exhaust types · 1-in baffle air space · bath fans to outdoors

> **HOW TO REASON THROUGH STEEP-SLOPE ITEMS**
> 1) **Trace a drop of water.** Whichever answer makes water flow over, never under, a lap is right. 2) **Find what can move.** Flashings attach to one element only (roof *or* wall), and metal needs clips. 3) **"Most likely cause"** items: match where and when the symptom appears to the installation error that produces exactly that. 4) When code and manufacturer differ, **the stem's wording tells you which authority** the item is asking about.
