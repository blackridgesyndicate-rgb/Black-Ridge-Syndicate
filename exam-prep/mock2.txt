### Q
S: 1
T: Low-slope roof wider than 50 ft
Q: A crew is replacing the membrane on a low-slope warehouse roof that is 160 ft wide. Which fall-protection arrangement is permitted for the roofing work?
A: A warning line system combined with a safety monitoring system for workers between the line and the edge
B: A safety monitoring system alone
C: A warning line system alone
D: Verbal reminders from the foreman at the start of each shift
ANS: A
EXP: On a low-slope roof wider than 50 ft, a safety monitor alone isn't permitted. The permitted options include guardrails, nets, or PFAS, or a warning line combined with one of those systems or with a safety monitor.
WB: A monitor alone is limited to roofs 50 ft or less in width.
WC: A warning line is never a stand-alone system.
WD: Verbal reminders aren't a fall-protection system.
SRC: 29 CFR 1926.501(b)(10)

### Q
S: 1
T: Safety monitor duties
Q: Which arrangement VIOLATES OSHA's requirements for a safety monitor?
A: The monitor watches the crew from the parking lot and warns them by two-way radio
B: The monitor is a competent person on the same roof surface as the crew
C: The monitor is close enough to warn workers by voice
D: The monitor has no other job duties while monitoring
ANS: A
EXP: A safety monitor must be on the same walking/working surface, within visual sighting distance of the employees, and close enough to communicate orally. A monitor in the parking lot with a radio doesn't meet this.
WB: This is a requirement.
WC: This is a requirement.
WD: This is a requirement: no other responsibilities that could distract from monitoring.
SRC: 29 CFR 1926.502(h)(1)

### Q
S: 1
T: Fall clearance calculation
Q: A roofer uses a 6-ft shock-absorbing lanyard attached to an anchor at the height of his back D-ring. Allow 3.5 ft of deceleration, 1 ft of harness stretch, 5 ft from the D-ring to the feet, and a 2-ft safety margin. The ground is 14 ft below the anchor. Is the clearance adequate?
A: No; about 17.5 ft of clearance is needed
B: Yes; only 9.5 ft is needed
C: Yes; only 12 ft is needed
D: No; about 25 ft is needed
ANS: A
EXP: Required clearance = 6 (free fall) + 3.5 (deceleration) + 1 (stretch) + 5 (D-ring to feet) + 2 (margin) = 17.5 ft. With only 14 ft, the worker could strike the ground. A self-retracting lifeline or different anchoring is needed.
WB: 9.5 ft counts only free fall and deceleration.
WC: 12 ft leaves out the body length below the D-ring and the margin.
WD: 25 ft overstates the requirement.
SRC: 29 CFR 1926.502(d)(16) (no contact with lower level; 6-ft free fall; 3.5-ft deceleration); ANSI Z359 clearance method

Q: According to OSHA and NIOSH nail gun guidance, which trigger type provides the greatest protection against unintended firing and double-fire injuries?
A: Full sequential trigger
B: Contact (bump) trigger
C: Single-shot contact trigger
D: Any trigger, if the operator wears safety glasses
ANS: A
